# Plan: map controls

Status: plan, 2026-10-21, **for review**. Design phase: **what the component offers, not how it is coded.** Nothing here is built. It comes from an app that wants the demo's controls in its own map window without copying them.

## What it is

**One controls view, all in one box**, beside `FantasyMapView`. It edits the settings that make and show a map, and **only edits them**: the app decides what to do with a change (make a new map, grow the realms again, redraw). The package's demo uses the same view, so there is one copy of the controls and a new option appears in the demo and in every app together.

It is **one view with one optional setting**: which groups to show. By default it shows all of them; an app that does not want some passes the ones it wants. There is no kit of pieces to assemble.

## The groups

- **Seed and land shape:** the seed (typed, or another at random) and the fourteen land shapes.
- **Detail:** the number of cells.
- **Coast:** smoothing, ink width, and the rough coast with its amplitude and variant.
- **Rivers:** the switch and the width.
- **Realms:** the settings that make them (see below), and whether they are drawn.
- **Pins:** the marker (map pin or dot), the colour, and how many pins there are.

**What stays in the demo, and is not in the component:** saving and opening files, the saved picture, and the raw-cell diagnostic overlay.

## How it behaves

- **A settings value in, the same value out.** The app owns it. The view tells the app *which part changed*, so that the app can do the right amount of work: a new **seed, shape or number of cells** needs a new map (the pins go with the old one); a **realm setting** grows the realms again on the same land and keeps the pins; a **coast or river setting** only redraws.
- **Defaults make a finished map:** rivers on, realms on (with `RealmDefaults`), the demo's coast, 10,000 cells. An app that hides a group gets its defaults and nothing to wire. *(The demo used to start with rivers and realms off; it now starts as the defaults do. See decision 1.)*
- **Nothing is stored by the component.** The seed, shape, cells and realm recipe are already in a saved map; the look settings are the app's to keep or not.

## An app's use

The app that asked shows **seed and land shape, realms and pins** only. The cells, coast and rivers are fixed at their defaults (10,000 cells, the default coast, rivers always on), so those groups are hidden and no look settings are kept with a map.

## Realm settings (decided 2026-10-21)

**The Realms group shows the number of realms (automatic or a number), how uneven the sizes are and the reach, in the open;** the typical realm size, how much hills, mountains and sea crossings slow growth, and the realm seed sit under one "More" disclosure inside the same group. The all-in-one view stays short, with no extra setting.

## Build order

**Decision 1, settled 2026-10-21: the defaults are the finished map** (rivers on, realms on; the demo starts from them and can switch either off). The question was: the defaults. The demo starts with rivers and realms off; an app that hides those groups needs them on. *Recommendation:* the settings' defaults are the finished map (rivers on, realms on), and the demo starts from them. The author can still switch either off in the demo.

1. **Built 2026-10-21.** **The settings type** (`MapSettings`, public, value semantics, `Hashable` and `Sendable`): seed, land shape, cells; coast (smoothing, ink width, rough coast, amplitude, variant); rivers (shown, width); realms (drawn, reach, automatic count or a number, realm seed, typical size, hill, mountain and crossing factors, how uneven); the pin marker and colour. It **validates** itself (out-of-range or not-finite values are refused with a named reason). `RealmDefaults` becomes the realm part of its defaults. *Tests:* the defaults; every refusal; value equality.

   *Built as:* `MapSettings` with nested `Coast`, `Rivers`, `Realms` and `Pin` values (seed `"12345"`, `continents`, 10,000 cells; the realm count and realm seed are optional, `nil` meaning automatic and the map's own seed) and `validate()` throwing `MapSettings.Problem.invalid(name)` for the first setting it cannot use. The limits are the package's own where it has them (the template keys, `CoastOutlines.maximumSmoothing`, `RoughnessSettings`' validation, `GrowthPolicy`'s factor and unevenness limits, `RealmDefaults.maximumRealms`, `PinLimits`' key length) and otherwise chosen: cells 1,000 to 100,000, a seed up to 200 characters, an ink width 0 to 10, a river width 0.05 to 10 times, a reach 0 to 10,000, a typical realm size above 0 and up to 10⁹. Five tests in `MapSettingsTests` (the defaults, **49 refusals, each with its setting's name**, the edges of every range accepted, all fourteen shapes accepted, equality and hashing by every setting). **Mutation checks (28 mutants of the ranges and checks): all caught.** Both release suites pass.
2. **Built 2026-10-21.** **What changed:** `MapSettings.change(from:)` says **new map** (seed, shape, cells), **realms** (any realm setting) or **look** (coast, rivers, whether realms are drawn). *Tests:* a table of every field and its change, and a test that **every stored field is classified** (a new field cannot be added without a decision).

   *Built as:* `MapSettings.Change`, an option set of `.newMap`, `.realms` and `.look`, and `change(from: old)` on the new settings; a change can need more than one, and the pin marker and colour need none (they are the next pin's). **The classification follows the groups, so a setting added to the coast, rivers or realms groups is classified by its group;** a new top-level setting is not, and the test catches it. Whether the realms are drawn is only a `.look` (they are made either way). A seed is compared as written, so one that differs only by spaces counts as another; an app that trims seeds trims before it compares. Six tests in `MapSettingsChangeTests`: a table of all 21 settings with the change each takes, in both directions, and a check that the table lists **every setting found by looking inside the value** (a new one fails the test until it is classified); nothing changed takes nothing; several changes are all reported; drawing the realms off is only a look; the three kinds are distinct and do not overlap. **Mutation checks (12 mutants): 10 caught at the first run; the two survivors, two kinds given the same bit, were closed by the distinct-and-disjoint test.** Both release suites pass.
3. **Built 2026-10-21.** **Applying the settings, without any view:** make a whole map from settings (the one-call helper takes them in place of its constants); grow the realms again on the same land (the heights, the rivers and the pins are untouched); the `MapStyle` a settings value draws with. *Tests:* the same settings give the same map; a realm change keeps the land, rivers and pins and changes the realms; the style follows the settings; the existing one-call tests still pass.

   *Built as:* `LandMap.addingRealms(settings:style:)` (a chosen or automatic number of realms, the reach, the typical size, the growth factors, how uneven, the realm seed; the colours resolved and saved; the older `addingRealms(realmSeed:style:)` calls it with the defaults); `WaterMap.generate(settings:)` (validates, then land, realms and water); `WaterMap.regrowingRealms(settings:)` (**the realm layer replaced and nothing else**: the land, the rivers, the climate and the pins are untouched, and the result is byte-for-byte the map `generate(settings:)` makes with those realm settings); `MapSettings.style` (the standard style with the coast, the river width and whether the realms are drawn); and `MapDrawing(water:settings:)` (rivers only when the settings show them). In the core, **`WaterMap.adding(_ layer:)`** (the water-map twin of `LandMap.adding`, the way `settingPins` is). Thirteen tests in `MapSettingsApplyTests`: the default settings draw with the standard style; the style follows each setting; rivers shown only when asked; the same settings give the same map and match the old one-call result; shape and seed decide the land; **a chosen number of realms is what you get** (3, 6 and 9) with its colours; the realm settings reach the recipe; **each realm setting changes the realms on its own** (the crossing factor on an archipelago, where realms span the sea); settings that cannot be used make nothing; realms made again equal a whole map with those settings, keep the land, rivers, climate and pins, and save and open identically; refused settings change nothing; `adding` swaps a layer without touching the water and refuses a layer that does not fit. **Mutation checks (24 mutants): 18 caught at the first run; six survived** (the reach, typical size, hill, mountain, crossing and unevenness reaching the growth: the recipe only records what was asked, and a changed realm seed hid the effect), **closed by the per-setting test and all caught on the rerun.** Both release suites pass. Not run in debug.
4. **Built 2026-10-21 (compiled and rendered in tests, not looked at: the author looks).** **The controls view** (`MapControls`): the groups above, each shown or hidden by one `groups` setting (default all), **Another Seed**, the realm settings with "More", and the pin marker and colour. It edits a binding and calls nothing. *Tests:* rendered with each group on its own, to show the groups appear and hide and that nothing it draws changes the settings; the author looks at it.

   *Built as:* `MapControls(settings:groups:pinCount:)` in `FantasyMapKit`, a plain column of controls with no scrolling or background of its own (an app puts it in a `ScrollView` or a sidebar), and `MapControls.Groups`, an option set of `seedAndShape`, `detail`, `coast`, `rivers`, `realms`, `pins` (default `.all`), shown in that order with a divider between. The controls and their ranges are the demo's: the seed with **Another Seed** and the fourteen land shapes; the cells (1,000, 3,000, 10,000, 30,000, 100,000, and the settings' own if it is another); smoothing, ink width, rough coast with its amplitude and variant; rivers and their width; **realms: draw realms, automatic number of realms or a number, how uneven, and the reach in the open, with the typical size (only for the automatic count), the hill, mountain and sea crossing costs and the realm seed under "More"**; and the pins' marker, the six colours and how many pins there are. Added to `MapSettings`: `randomSeed()` and `Realms.automaticCount` (turning it off asks for the number last asked for, or 8). Eight tests in `MapControlsTests`: the six groups are distinct and `.all` is every one; each group alone and no groups at all (nothing drawn); hiding any group makes the controls shorter and showing more taller; a chosen number adds its stepper; the pin count line; **drawing the controls writes no setting**; another seed is a number of 6 to 9 digits that passes validation and differs each time; the automatic-count switch. **Mutation checks (14 mutants): 11 caught; three survived and are not closed:** the lower bound of the random seed (a seed below six digits would still be valid, and the chance of drawing one is one in ten thousand, so a test cannot see it without injecting the random numbers), the divider between groups (a divider is about a point tall and the spacing hides it in a height test), and the extra cell count when the settings' own is not one of the five (a picker with an unlisted choice looks the same). Both release suites pass. Not run in debug.
5. **Built 2026-10-21 (compiled and rendered in tests; typing, the colours and the delete are the author's to try).** **The pin note** (the sticky-note popover and its colour row) moves from the demo into the package as a public view, so an app does not copy it. *Tests:* the colours it offers and that editing changes the pin it was given.
   *Built as:* `PinNote(note:color:onDelete:)` in `FantasyMapKit`: the memo to write, the six colours and a delete, on a light yellow note; it edits what it is given and the app keeps the pin. **It cuts the memo to what a map can save** through the new `PinLimits.limiting(note:)` in the core (2,000 characters and 16,000 UTF-8 bytes, dropping whole characters so a multi-part emoji is never split): **the demo only cut the characters, so, reading its code, a memo of 700 family emoji (17,500 bytes) would have been accepted by the editor and refused when the map was saved** (not reproduced in the running demo); that is fixed by moving the limit into the shared view. Also `PinGeometry.markerFrame(of:in:viewSize:viewport:markers:)`, which says where a pin's marker is in a view (nil when it is not placeable or not in view), for an app to anchor the note's popover; the demo used its own arithmetic for this and now calls it. Nine tests: four on the note limit in the core (a memo that fits is returned as it is, one over the character limit is cut, one over the byte limit is cut to whole characters, and what it keeps is always within both limits and stable), four on the frame (a map pin's tip and a dot's centre are on the place, it equals the placement the view draws, out of view or not placeable has none, a custom marker's size and anchor are used) and one that the note draws and drawing it edits nothing. The demo now uses `PinNote` and `markerFrame`. **Mutation checks (9 mutants): 8 caught, one of them only because it did not compile; the survivor is the note's text binding using the limit, which only typing in the view can show.** Both release suites pass. Not run in debug.

6. **Built 2026-10-21 (compiled in debug and release; not run: the author looks).** **The demo adopts it:** its controls become `MapControls` over `MapSettings` (its own file buttons, the saved picture and the diagnostic overlay stay), and its pins use the shared note. *Check:* the demo behaves as before, and the author looks at it.

   *Built as:* the demo's settings are now `DemoSettings { map: MapSettings, realmOverlay, showSeeds, showLinks }` (seed `kaz-1` as before); its whole controls column is `MapControls(settings: $settings.map, pinCount:)` followed by the demo's own: the raw-cell realm overlay with its seeds and links, Save picture, Save map, Open map, Fit and zoom, and the info popover. **The map is made by the package**: `WaterMap.generate(settings:)` when the rivers are shown, and otherwise the land with `addingRealms(settings:style:)`; the style is `MapSettings.style`, and the drawing `MapDrawing(water:settings:)`. The overlay is made from the same realm settings. **The demo now starts from the finished map** (rivers and realms on, as decision 1 says) and has **no master "Realms" toggle**: the realms are always made, and **Draw realms** only draws them. The next pin's marker and colour are in the settings and **change no map** (the demo makes the map again only when `settings.making`, the settings without the pin, changes). The demo file went from 564 to 422 lines (223 lines removed and 81 added, against the last commit), by dropping its own copies of the controls, the style and the realm steps. **Not done here:** the demo still makes the whole map again for any change; it does not use `MapSettings.change(from:)` to grow only the realms or only redraw, which an app can. **Not checked by running it:** I only built the demo; the author looks.
   **Added while using it in an app (2026-10-21): `MapSettings(describing: map)`**, which reads the settings back from a map (the seed, land shape and cells, and the realm recipe: the reach, the number asked for, the realm seed with "the map's own" read back as nil, the typical size, the growth factors and how uneven; everything a map does not keep is the default). Two tests (settings made, described and equal; a map without realms; applying the described settings again gives the same map), and **mutation checks: 13 mutants, all caught.** An app opens a map by describing it and then edits those settings.

7. **Built 2026-10-21 (the screenshot is the author's to take).** **Docs and the guide:** a "Controls" section with a short example, the README, and a new demo screenshot (the author's).

   *Built as:* a "Controls, settings and the pin note" section in `docs/programming-guide.md` (the settings, `MapControls` with its groups, what a change takes with a full `apply()` example, `describing`, `PinNote` and `markerFrame`), a sentence in the README's drawing paragraph, and the guide's code **compiled against the package in a scratch package** (the full `apply()` example, a view with the controls, the pin note in a popover anchored with `markerFrame`; compiled, not run). The demo screenshot in the README still shows the old controls and is the author's to retake.

**Evidence boundary:** steps 1 to 3 are tested logic; steps 4 to 6 are views, tested only as far as rendering goes, and the author looks at them. Mutation checks on steps 1 to 3 (they are validation and classification logic); none on the views. Shipped as 0.2.0 only when the author says the package has settled.

## Not in this plan

- Any control the demo does not already have.
- Naming and the exact shape of the settings type, which are decided when it is built.
- A release: the package is at 0.1.0 and the rename and the one-call helper are on `develop`; this ships in 0.2.0 only when the author says the package has settled.
