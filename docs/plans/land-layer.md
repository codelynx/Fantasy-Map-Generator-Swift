# Port Plan, Layer 1: Land

Status: plan, amended after review 2026-10-20, for the author's approval. Written after reading the original's land code closely (clone at commit `8936c9c`, 2026-10-19). This is the first layer of [the design](../design.md): a map of cells with heights, sea, coast, land, islands and lakes. **Nothing is built.** This plan says what the original does, what the Swift package would hold and in what order it would be built, and what is still open.

## What was read, and what was not

Read line by line: `grid-generator.ts` (grid, jitter, boundary points, deep-depression lakes, near-sea lakes), `voronoi.ts` (cells and vertices from a Delaunay triangulation), most of `heightmap-generator.ts` (Hill, Pit, Range, the Strait's setup, `modify`, `smooth`, `mask`, `invert`, the template runner), `data/heightmap-templates.ts` (the format and the first templates), and `features-generator.ts`'s `markupGrid` and distance field. Read only at the level of its outline: the Trough step (a mirror of Range), the middle of Strait, `lakes.ts`, `ocean-generator.ts`, `resample.ts`, `pack-generator.ts`, and the image-to-heightmap path. **Nothing was run or compared yet.** Sizes are the original's line counts, not our effort.

## What the original does for the land

1. **Points.** A square grid of points, each shifted at random by up to 90 % of half the spacing; the spacing is `sqrt(width × height / cells wanted)`. Along the map edge it adds pseudo-points, which clip the outer cells and get no cells of their own.
2. **Cells.** A Delaunay triangulation of the points (the Delaunator library), turned into Voronoi cells: for each cell its neighbour cells, its corner vertices, and whether it touches the border.
3. **Heights.** One whole number from 0 to 100 per cell, with **20 as sea level** (land is height ≥ 20). A template is a short script, one step per line. The steps are `Hill`, `Pit`, `Range`, `Trough`, `Strait`, `Mask`, `Invert`, `Add`, `Multiply` and `Smooth`. Most take a count, a height and the x and y ranges as percentages of the map (for example `Hill 1 90-100 44-56 40-60`: one hill, height 90 to 100, centred 44 to 56 % across and 40 to 60 % down). There are fourteen templates (Volcano, High Island, Low Island, Continents, Archipelago, Atoll, Mediterranean, Peninsula, Pangea, Isthmus, Shattered, Taklamakan, Old World, Fractious), each with a probability weight, and a map can also take its heights from a picture.
4. **Features.** A flood fill that numbers every connected body of sea or land, and calls land an island, border-touching water an ocean and enclosed water a lake. It also marks, for each cell, its distance from the coast (positive on land, negative in water, to −10).
5. **Extra lakes.** Land hollows that water could not drain out of become lakes (height 19), and lakes next to the sea that would break through a low ridge are opened to the ocean.

## Decisions taken after review (2026-10-20)

- **Exact parity with the original, at a pinned original version, is a development target, not a public promise.** It is the plan's main test oracle. If it proves impractical, the package chooses deterministic Swift behaviour **explicitly**, records the decision and says what no longer matches; it never switches silently. (The design note was corrected to say so.)
- **Port the real dependencies, not substitutes.** The original imports the npm packages `alea` and `delaunator`, not the copies under `public/libs`. The Swift port ports **Alea** and **Delaunator** (with its **robust-predicates** dependency), pins the versions used for the reference fixtures, and carries their notices: alea 1.0.1 (MIT), delaunator 5.1.0 (ISC), robust-predicates 3.0.3 (Unlicense), as found in the original's installed copies; each licence file is read and recorded before any code is ported. A home-made triangulator is **not** planned: a different valid triangulation, or a different neighbour order, would change everything generated afterwards. No fallback generator is planned either.
- **Compare in stages, never only the final map.** Fixtures are generated by the original and compared at each stage: the random outputs, the interior and boundary points, the triangulation and each cell's ordered neighbours, the heights after each step, then features and lakes. An early mismatch must be locatable.

## What a Swift version would hold (what, not how)

- **A land map:** its seed and settings; the cells' points **and the boundary points** (cells are built from both); for each cell its neighbours and its corner points; the heights; and for each cell whether it is sea, coast or land, which body it belongs to, and its graph distance from the coast; plus the list of bodies (ocean, lake, island) and their kinds.
- **Settings for this layer:** the size, how many cells, and a land shape. A land shape is either one of the named templates or **an editable list of steps** in the original's step format (kept readable, because it is also what lets an artist adjust the land).
- **Saved (the schema is defined with the basic types, step 1; the code that writes it comes last):** a format version; an algorithm revision; the dimensions and the grid settings; the interior and boundary points; and the final heights, in their order. The points and final heights are the source of truth. The cells' neighbours and corners can be rebuilt from the points, as the original's saved files do. **Rebuilding must never rerun lake generation or change saved heights, and an upgraded algorithm must never silently change an old map's topology on load.**

## Points the port must get right

- **Byte storage, stated carefully.** The original keeps heights in a byte array (`Uint8Array`). Storing a finite fractional value there **truncates toward zero and wraps modulo 256**; it is **not** a clamp to 0 to 100. The 0 to 100 limit comes from the algorithm's own `lim` calls, and intermediate values are floating-point. Swift's ordinary `UInt8` conversion is not a drop-in replacement. The port keeps three things apart: the algorithm's explicit range limiting, JavaScript's storage conversion, and the intermediate floating-point values, and applies the conversion at the actual assignment points. Tested separately with fractional values, negatives, overflow and non-finite input, and then with representative height steps against the original.
- **Random state is explicit and local.** The original reseeds at grid generation, again at height generation, and again at feature markup (it is not one unbroken stream for the whole pipeline), and it does so by replacing a global. The port holds **generation-local state with explicit resets at the same points**, and no global, since it is meant for concurrent use. Seed strings: fixtures include empty, ASCII and non-ASCII seeds, because the original's seed hashing may depend on UTF-16 code units.
- **Parity depends on more than the seed.** Reset points, numeric conversions, rounding, traversal order, triangulation tie-breaking and floating-point arithmetic all matter, and each is a place the port can drift.
- **Step arguments are text** in the original (`"90-100"`, `"1-2"`, `"all"`, `"land"`); the port parses them once into proper values.
- **The distance field is narrower than I first said.** `markupGrid` marks land coast cells (+1) and water coast cells (−1) and then propagates **water** distances (−2 down to −10); it does not compute a positive distance inland. Layer 1 matches that field exactly; a full land-distance calculation, if wanted, is a separate addition. It is a **graph distance** (steps between neighbouring cells), not a distance in map units.
- **The Strait step** builds its path without the visited-cell check the Range step has. It is carried over unchanged unless a decision is made otherwise.

## Build order, each step tested before the next

Before step 1 (**done 2026-10-20**): the dependency versions and licence texts are recorded in `docs/dependencies.md`, and a **small fixture is captured from the original** by `tools/capture-fixtures.mjs` into `Tests/FantasyMapKitTests/Fixtures/original/` (Alea values for nine seeds, including empty, ASCII, non-ASCII, a surrogate pair and composed versus decomposed "é"; and, for three tiny grids, the points, boundary points and Delaunator's triangles). The points in `grid.json` are re-implemented from the original's source, not run from its own code, which the fixtures' README says.

1. **Basic types, the saved schema, and Alea.** *(done; [step page](../steps/01-types-and-alea.md))* Whole-number heights, a point, the settings, the schema in the list above, and the port of Alea. Tested against the random outputs of the original.
2. **The points.** *(done; [step page](../steps/02-grid-points.md))* Spacing, jittered interior points, boundary points. Tested against the original's points; and: the count is near the cells wanted, every point is inside the map.
3. **The triangulation and the cells *(done; [step page](../steps/03-triangulation-and-cells.md))* (Delaunator and robust-predicates, then the Voronoi cells).** Tested against the original's ordered neighbours on a tiny grid and a normal grid; and: neighbours are mutual, cell polygons are closed, border cells are marked, duplicate and collinear points are handled.
4. **The height operations.** *(done: every operation (`Hill`, `Pit`, `Range`, `Trough`, `Strait`, `Add`, `Multiply`, `Smooth`, `Mask`, `Invert`); [step page](../steps/04-height-operations.md))* Hill, Pit, Range, Trough, Strait, Mask, Invert, Add, Multiply, Smooth. **Start with one small end-to-end template**, then the others. Each operation is compared against the original, including the storage conversion; and: range limiting, the sea-level values 19 and 20, malformed step input and bounded settings.
5. **The remaining templates and the runner.** *(done 2026-10-20; [step page](../steps/04-height-operations.md))* The fourteen scripts and the step parser. Compared against the original on representative seeds; the valid invariants are defined from those, and **not** assumed (not every template yields both land and sea for every seed and configuration).
6. **Features and the distance field.** *(done 2026-10-20; [step page](../steps/06-features.md))* As `markupGrid` defines them. Tested on a hand-made small map, an all-land map and an all-water map, and against the original.
7. **Lakes.** *(done 2026-10-20; [step page](../steps/07-lakes.md))* Features come first, as in the original. The complete depression and near-sea lake routines are read before porting; they change heights and the coast and feature marks, so the tests check that these still agree afterwards. Climate-dependent lake behaviour stays out of this layer.
8. **Saving and loading.** *(done 2026-10-20; [step page](../steps/08-saving-and-loading.md))* the code for the schema defined in step 1. A loaded map equals the one saved, and loading does not regenerate lakes or alter heights.
   **Decided 2026-10-20 (after the review of step 7): a saved land map holds the exact state.** Beside the points and the final heights it saves the feature ids, the feature records (including records no cell refers to any more, such as a lake that was opened into an ocean) and the coast-distance field, so a loaded map is identical to the one saved and loading recomputes nothing and never re-runs lake creation. The file is larger by about three bytes a cell. Freshly canonicalised features on load, and re-running the lakes from pre-lake heights, were set aside.

Image-to-heightmap waits. Each step gets a short documentation page in this repository.

## Open questions for the author (all answered 2026-10-20; the minimum and maximum cell counts remain to be set at step 4)

1. **Parity as a target:** the reviewer's answer is yes, as a target and not a promise, with no fallback generator; this needs the author's confirmation.
2. ~~Which land shapes are in the first public version.~~ **Decided 2026-10-20: all fourteen templates**, each with an original-generated fixture (the operations are shared, so the extra cost is mainly the fixtures).
3. **Heights from a picture:** later, as the review advises.
4. ~~Cell count and map size.~~ **Decided 2026-10-20 (changed from the first answer): any number of cells the app likes.** The original's 13 density steps (1,000 to 100,000, default step 4 = 10,000) stay as **named presets**, and parity with the original is tested at those. The original's own rule is kept for other counts: its blob-power and line-power tables are keyed by exactly those counts, and any other count falls back to generic values (0.98 and 0.81), so a count between two steps behaves as the original would, not as a tuned value. Interpolating the tuning between steps would be a deliberate change from the original and is not planned. **The default is step 4, 10,000 cells, as in the original (the author's choice, 2026-10-20, corrected from an earlier "step 13").** Step 13 (100,000 cells) stays a named preset, and a client can pass any count. The cost of the larger sizes in generation time, saved size and drawing has not been measured for a Swift version; it is to be measured at step 4 (cells) of the build and written into the documentation as guidance. Sensible limits (a minimum and a maximum) are still to be set, and the documentation says which sizes suit which devices.
5. ~~Robust arithmetic.~~ **Decided 2026-10-20: port `orient2d` and its helpers from `robust-predicates` exactly as the library has them** (about 320 lines including helpers), so the triangulation is the same as the original's in the same cases. Delaunator calls `orient2d` in four places; its other geometric test, `inCircle`, is its own floating-point code and is ported as written. The other predicates in the library (`incircle`, `insphere`, `orient3d`) are not used and not ported. The Unlicense notice is recorded.

## Not in this layer

Climate, rivers, terrain types, realms, names and drawing. This plan does not start any of them.

## What this plan does not claim

The effort and the line counts are estimates. The step-by-step behaviour above is from reading the code and has not been checked by running the original or the port.
