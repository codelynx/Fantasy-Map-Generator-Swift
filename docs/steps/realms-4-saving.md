# Realms, saving: the realm layer in the land file

Built 2026-10-21, from [the plan](../plans/realms-saving.md) (decisions: the realm layer lives in the land map's own file, as the design decided, and **the resolved realm colours are saved with the realms**, so a map opened again shows the colours it was saved with). `LandMap.adding(_:)` attaches a `RealmLayer`; `encoded()` and `init(decoding:)` save and load it.

## What it does

- **The layer** (`Realms/RealmLayer.swift`): the **ownership** (the realm of every cell: 1 to K on land, 0 on water, and the realm records: number, group, seed cell, cell count, area), the **recipe** (realm seed, reach, the count asked for or nil for automatic, the resolved count, the typical area, the maximum realms, the sample size, the hill, mountain and crossing factors, the unevenness: provenance, **never used to regenerate on load**), and **one resolved colour per realm** (`RealmColor`: red, green and blue, 0 to 1, plain numbers so that the core needs no drawing code; nil when none were saved). The colours are kept apart from the owner data.
- **The file:** `SavedLandMap` gets an optional `realms`. **A map with realms is written in format version 2; a map without realms is written in version 1, byte for byte as before** (the existing fixtures and files are unchanged), and both versions are read. The version says whether there is a layer (a version 1 file with a layer, or a version 2 file without one, is refused). A file with no realm layer is **a distinct valid state: a map with no realms, not a map with one realm.** The water map embeds the land map, so it carries the layer too (and its compressed form).
- **Loading and attaching validate, and recompute nothing that is saved.** The same check serves `LandMap.adding(_:)` and loading: the recipe's ranges by the generators' own rules and the counts (the resolved count equals the number of realms and the chosen count, if any, **and does not pass the recipe's maximum number of realms**); the owner array (one value per cell, every land cell a realm 1 to K, every water cell 0, the realms numbered 1, 2, 3 in order); each realm record against the cells (the cell count, the **area, summed in cell order as growth sums it, so it is compared exactly**, a seed cell in the map that the realm owns, and its group); **each realm connected over land and the permitted sea crossings**: the land is grouped again from the map and the saved reach, **the links between landmasses are verified as over-water crossings** (growth's own verification, `RealmOwnership.verifyCrossings`: each link a genuine witness and a clean crossing, within an explicit budget of tests), and the ownership's own independent check runs over land and those links (a validation, not a regeneration). **The work is bounded by three things, not by the grouping's budgets alone:** the grouping's own budgets, the crossing verification's budget, and one pass over the cells, their neighbours and the links, **linear whatever the number of realms** (the owners are counted once and each realm's search visits only the cells it owns; an earlier version scanned every cell once per realm); and the colours (one per realm, each channel 0 to 1). A layer that does not fit changes nothing.
- **The drawing:** a map with a layer draws its realms with the saved colours, **used on their own whatever the style's palette is** (the palette is asked, and validated, only when colours are derived; the realms are hidden by `MapStyle.showRealms` or a wash opacity of 0, not by the palette). A layer with no saved colours takes the colours the drawing derives, **which may change with the colouring rule until they are saved**; if the realms cannot be drawn, `MapDrawing.realmProblem` says why. `MapDrawing.resolvedRealmColors(for:ownership:style:)` gives the colours an app saves. **A map opened again draws the same picture as before it was saved, with the same renderer and presentation settings** (tested for a land map and a water map). **A saved map is data, not the whole picture:** the wash opacity, the border, the coast smoothing and the palette are the style's and can change independently of the ownership and the colours; a saved presentation preset would be a separate, versioned feature.
- **The demo:** with "Draw realms in the picture" on, the realms are attached to the map with the colours they are drawn in, so "Save map…" keeps them; "Open map…" reads a land or water map and shows its realms in the saved colours until a setting changes, **and shows the saved recipe (seed, reach, count mode, resolved count) as provenance only**, not as evidence that today's generators would make the same realms; it starts nothing.

## Tests

`RealmLayerTests` (15, core) and 5 new in `RealmDrawingTests`: round trips on all 14 templates with automatic and chosen counts and several unevenness settings (the loaded layer equals the saved one, saving again gives the same bytes, and **the loaded ownership equals the realms grown again from the saved recipe**); a map without realms is written as version 1 with no `realms` key and loads with none; the colours are saved with and kept apart from the owner data; a water map carries the realms (and compressed); the version says whether there is a layer (four mismatches); the owner array and every record check (a wrong length, a land cell with no realm or a missing or negative one, a water cell with a realm, a wrong numbering, a wrong count, a zero count, a wrong or non-finite area, a seed cell outside the map, **including when the first or last cell is the realm's own**, a seed cell of another realm, a wrong group); a realm that is not connected is refused **with records made consistent, so that connectivity and not the records refuses it**; every recipe range and the reach (a reach that groups the land differently is refused on a map of several islands); the colours (a missing or extra colour, NaN, below 0, above 1, infinite); an attached layer that does not fit changes nothing, and another map's layer is refused; two maps differ when their realms, colours or recipe differ; corrupt JSON fails to decode; **a fixed version-2 file** (`Fixtures/realmsrevision2`, committed once and never regenerated by a test, rewritten only deliberately with `MAP_WRITE_REALMS_REVISION2=<folder> swift test --filter RealmLayerTests/writeTheFixture`) loads, re-saves to the same bytes and equals the map and realms made today. Drawing: the saved colours are used as they are, an opened map draws the same picture, the switch, realms that cannot be drawn are reported, and the resolved colours are one per realm and the ones the drawing uses.

**Mutation checks:** 30 mutants of the layer, the saved record and `LandMap`: 22 caught at the first run; the others closed by a test (an infinite reach, equality that includes the realms, the clamped seed index when the first or last cell is the realm's) or by removing a check that was redundant (a cell count of at least 1 and an area that is finite follow from the equalities; the per-cell group check follows from connectivity and the seed's group; the colour's finiteness follows from the range comparisons), then rerun: none survive. **A second round after the review's corrections** (the linear check, the verified crossings, the cap, the saved colours): 10 mutants, 9 caught at once and the tenth (the guard against an owner outside the realms) after a direct test of the ownership check with invalid owners. The count of work is an instrumented counter over the operations the check does, not a clock; a regression that added an uncounted scan would not show in it, which the test cannot rule out.

## Compatibility

Loading rebuilds the groups, the crossings, the clipped polygons and the areas **with the current code**, and compares the saved records with them. A change to the grouping, the clipping, the crossing verification or the area arithmetic would therefore make an old layer fail validation, and the recipe being provenance does not remove that dependency. Any such change needs a revision bump with the old calculation kept (or an explicit refusal of old layers), and the fixed version-2 file, which loads and re-saves to the same bytes, is the guard.

## What it does not show

The realm cleanup (step 4), the reach and typical-area defaults, and editing realms are not built, so a realm layer is what the generators make now. The colours are the ones the drawing derived when saved; a "reassign colours" action is not built. The wash opacity, the border and the palette are not saved (the style's). Validation proves a saved layer is consistent with its map, not that it is the one a recipe would grow again (the recipe is provenance, and a test shows today's generators reproduce the fixed file).
