# Drawing the realms, stage 1: wash and borders

Built 2026-10-21, from [the plan](../plans/drawing-realms.md), after reading the original's `draw-states.ts`, `draw-borders.ts`, `assignColors` and its style files ("i respect original where possible"). `MapDrawing(map:ownership:style:)` and `MapDrawing(water:ownership:style:)` (both throwing) draw a realm map's wash and borders; `FantasyMapView` and `MapImage` show them; `FantasyMapDemo` has "Draw realms in the picture".

## What it does

- **One partition for the wash and the borders** (`RealmDrawing.extendedOwners`): a land cell keeps its realm; a water cell beside owned land takes the realm of its **nearest** owned land neighbour (these cells lie under the displayed coast wherever smoothing turns water into land, so no sliver of land is left bare); every other water cell has none. The wash colours and the border lines both come from this one array, so **a border is exactly where the wash changes colour** and always separates two different washes.
- **The wash:** a soft colour per realm, drawn as **opaque polygons in one group at the wash opacity (default 0.3, the middle of the original's 0.1 to 0.45)**, so that overlaps do not darken, clipped to the displayed land outline (the same clip as the land fill, the rivers and the ink), under the rivers. **The wash is opaque by construction:** a palette colour's own alpha is dropped and `realmWashOpacity` is the only transparency control.
- **The borders:** chains of the cells' own vertices along every edge between two cells of different realms of the partition, as the original traces them (straight lines through the raw vertices; chains end at junctions of three or more and at the frame), **dashed, thin, grey** (the author's decision, following the original, which dashes in every style read; colour, width and dash are style settings, an empty dash is a plain line). Each chain is one stroke, so the dashes run on through corners. A realm enclosed in another gets a closed loop. **Where a border reaches a coast it continues over the water cells beside the land** (the edges between such cells of two realms, and the raw coast edge where a water cell takes one realm's colour beside another realm's land) as far as the displayed coast reaches over them, and the land clip cuts the rest; an edge between a land cell and a water cell of the **same** realm is not a border, so nothing is drawn along a coast or the frame. Interior borders stay raw, as in the original. In a picture of one map the dashed border meets the coast ink at the junction.
- **Colours:** the original's rule: greedy colouring from six fixed soft colours (d3's Set2), the palette rotated by one per realm, so that realms that touch (anywhere the wash changes colour) differ; a realm that finds no free colour takes a seeded colour; a colour that several realms end with is mixed a little for each repeat. **A deterministic adaptation:** the original mixes with a random colour from its own rainbow scale and formats colours through d3; this package mixes 20% with a **seeded** random colour and brightens by d3's factor (1 / 0.7)^0.3, so the same realms always get the same colours but not the same ones the original would draw. Colours are told apart by **palette entry, not by value**, so a custom palette that repeats a colour can give neighbours equal values, the fallback colour is only unlikely to match a neighbour, and a palette with a channel that is not finite or not 0 to 1 is refused (`invalidPalette`).
- **Validation:** every land cell must name a valid realm, every water cell must be 0, and the realms are numbered 1, 2, 3 in order, or the drawing is refused (`ownershipDoesNotMatchMap`) and nothing is changed. `addRealms` is internal: the public initialisers build the land and the realms together, so a drawing and its map cannot be mismatched. If the coast cannot be trusted (its frame closure was inconsistent) the realms are left out, never painted over the sea.

## Tests

`RealmDrawingTests` (22): neighbouring realms differ in colour; the greedy colouring and the palette rotation against an independent expected sequence (a realm that touches realm 1 where the palette comes round skips its colour); the mixing arithmetic pinned; a palette that is not made of colours is refused without trapping; **the border segments are compared one by one, as a multiset of normalised endpoint pairs, with edges found independently** (an omitted and a duplicated edge cannot cancel); chains end at junctions and are single strokes (the stroke count equals the open chains plus the loops, worked out independently); a realm enclosed in another gives one closed loop of its own outline; every border edge separates two different wash colours and some chains run on from land into water; no open border end stops inside the displayed land away from the frame and from a junction; the wash is opaque whatever the palette alpha and overlapping polygons do not darken it; the style switches; ownership validation (a land cell with no realm, a water cell with a realm, a wrong numbering, a wrong length); the wash holds owned land and the water beside it in the nearest realm's colour; identical inputs give the same drawing; **whole-map wash and border lines show only on the land in both renderers; the dash pattern is applied by both**; the wash pixels equal the land colour blended at the wash opacity with the realm colour; the view and the image agree; an untrusted coast leaves the realms out.

**The SwiftUI renderer's output varies from render to render** (measured: the third render of one view differed from the second in about 11,000 channel values, and some runs differed by about 660 pixels outside the land), so the tests render each drawing three times through the view and use the last, and allow 1,500 pixels outside the land silhouette for the view. **That allowance is noise tolerance, not evidence of zero leakage**; a clip that does not work changes tens of thousands of pixels (the mutants that remove the view's wash clip and border clip both fail despite the allowance). The image renderer's tests allow none.

**Mutation checks:** several rounds, 26 mutants in the last, 24 caught; the two survivors were an equivalent check (water cells have no owner, so the land test beside it was redundant, and was removed) and the colouring's "touching" rule, which was changed to the partition's own adjacency (realms that show a border between them differ), the more correct rule. Earlier rounds found gaps (palette rotation and skipped colours, mixing, the dash in both renderers, the clips, junctions, open chains and loops) that now have tests of their own.

## How it looks (one picture, judged by eye)

A 10,000-cell `continents` map with water, rivers and 11 realms: a soft wash held inside the coast, thin grey dashed borders over the rivers, the coast ink on top. **One picture by one viewer:** the author's own look in the demo is the judgement that counts.

## What it does not show

No halo (the original's darker edge shade along a state, deferred as a style option), no water-gap stroke, no names. Borders are raw vertex chains, not smoothed like the coast. **The realm ownership and the realm settings are not saved in the file yet** (the reach and the typical area are provisional), so the demo's seed and settings are the only record of a realm picture; saving ownership and its presentation (the resolved colours, the palette, the opacity and border settings) is the next step.
