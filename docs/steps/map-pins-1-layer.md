# Pins on the map, stage 1

Built 2026-10-21, from [the plan](../plans/map-pins.md) (decisions: pins belong to the library's `FantasyMapView` as a layer the client opts into; **a pin keeps its size on the screen when the map is zoomed**; the markers may be like 凸 and the client can replace them and the behaviour; **the client owns the annotation and saves its pins separately from the framework's saved map**).

## What it does

- **`MapPin`:** an identifier the client chooses, a place in **map units** (the viewport's own), a marker key (`"pin"`, `"dot"`, or any key a client's own markers understand) and **a colour key** (`red`, `orange`, `yellow`, `green`, `blue` or `purple`, the standard markers' few light colours; an unknown key is red, and a pin saved without a colour reads as red). `Codable` as a convenience the client may use to save its pins; a pin whose place is not finite is never drawn or hit.
- **`PinLayer`** (given to `FantasyMapView(…, pinLayer:)`; without it the view shows no pins and behaves as before): the pins, an optional bound selected id, the markers, how a pin is asked for (`DropMode`: press and hold, the default; a click; or off), whether pins can be dragged, a hit slop, and an event handler.
- **Markers keep their own size on the screen at any zoom;** only their anchor follows the map. `PinMarkers` is what a client replaces: `layout(for:)` (the size in view points and the anchor inside the marker) and `view(for:isSelected:)`. **The standard markers** (`StandardPinMarkers`, with the palette, colours and sizes the client sets): **a map pin** (a teardrop with a light dot, anchored at its tip) and **a dot** (anchored at its centre); an unknown key draws as a map pin. **The selected pin keeps its colour and is shown by a white halo and a heavier outline.** **A 凸 tab marker was built and then removed at the author's request (2026-10-21, "for now we don't need it"); a client that wants one supplies it through its own `PinMarkers`.**
- **Events** (`PinEvent`), reported and **not acted on** (the framework changes none of the client's data): `dropped(at:)` (a place on the map, inside it), `selected(id:anchor:)` (where its anchor is in the view, for the client's own callout), `deselected`, `moved(id:to:)` (kept inside the map; the pin has not moved until the client moves it) and `removeRequested(id:)` (the Delete key). **The framework draws no annotation editor.**
- **Gestures:** a marker has its own tap (select) and drag (move), taking priority over the map's pan, so dragging a pin does not move the map; a press and hold (or a click, as the client chooses) on the map away from any pin drops one, inside the map only; a tap away from any pin deselects; the selected marker is drawn on top; Delete asks the client to remove the selected pin.
- **`PinGeometry`** (pure, tested): the placement of each pin for a viewport (the anchor, the frame, whether any of it is in view), the topmost pin under a point (the last in order among those visible, with the slop), the map point a drop lands on (nil in the margin around the map), and where a dragged pin lands (kept inside the map). An unusable geometry places nothing.
- **`LandMap.fingerprint`** (and `WaterMap.fingerprint`, the same geography): **a SHA-256 of the seed, the size, the cells asked for, the cell and boundary points, the final heights, the feature ids and the coast distance, as canonical bytes** (fixed-width little-endian numbers, doubles by their bit patterns), so it is the same on every platform and does not depend on the JSON encoder, written out in the core (`Hashing/SHA256.swift`, tested against the published vectors). Realms, water and the recipe are not part of it. **A client keeps its pins with the fingerprint of the map they were dropped on and can tell whether the map it is shown is the one they were made on, so pins are never silently moved to another picture.** About 20 ms at 100,000 cells. It is not a secret and not a file checksum.
- **The demo** is a client: it keeps its own `DemoPin` (the framework's pin plus a note) **per map fingerprint**, so another map shows its own pins and never these; **tapping a pin opens its memo as a popover beside it** (a light sticky note: a few lines of text, a few colours for the pin and a delete; the popover follows the pin as the map zooms and pans, and a newly dropped pin opens its memo at once); the side panel chooses how new pins look (map pin or dot, and a colour); and **"Save pins…" and "Open pins…" use a file of their own, apart from the map file, holding the map's fingerprint and the pins; a pins file for another map is refused.**

## Tests

`PinTests` (12), `PinViewTests` (7) and 6 more in `FingerprintTests`:
- **Placement:** a marker's size is the same at every zoom and only its anchor follows the map; the frame is positioned by the anchor for each marker; a pin out of view is not visible and one whose marker rises into the view is; pins that cannot be placed (a position that is not finite or overflows on the screen, a layout that is not a size or an anchor that is not finite, an unusable geometry) are left out.
- **Hit-testing:** the topmost of overlapping pins is hit; the slop grows a frame; a pin out of view is never hit; a map pin is hit above its tip, not below it.
- **Dropping and moving:** a drop lands on the map point under it, and not in the margin on any of the four sides; a dragged pin lands where it was dropped and stops at the map's edge.
- **Markers and shapes:** the anchors and sizes of the standard markers (an unknown marker is a map pin) and that sizes are the client's to set; the colour keys (six, an unknown key is red, a client's own palette); the pin value with a colour, and an old pin without one reading as red; the teardrop's head, point and empty corners.
- **Pictures:** a pin is drawn at its anchor and nowhere else (the changed pixels lie inside the marker's frame, the head is the marker's red); **a pin takes its colour from its key and an unknown key is red; the selected marker keeps its colour and gains a white halo outside its edge;** the marker has the same size at zoom 1 and 3 and stands on the anchor the map gives it when panned; a pin out of view draws nothing; the selected pin is on top of overlapping pins; a client's own marker, size and anchor replace the standard one; an empty layer draws nothing.
- **Fingerprint:** SHA-256 against the vectors (including the block-boundary lengths, checked against another implementation); the fingerprint is stable (the same map, a map saved and opened again), differs for another seed, land shape, number of cells or size, and for a change to a height, a point, the coast distance, the feature ids or the seed; realms and water do not change it.
- **The gestures (tap, drag, press and hold, click, Delete) cannot be driven from a test:** what they call is tested, and they need looking at in the demo. iOS touch is not verified, and how the pin gestures combine with the map's pan and pinch on a real trackpad has not been tried.

**Mutation checks:** 38 mutants of the pin geometry, the shapes, the markers, the overlay and the fingerprint: 24 caught at the first run; eight of the others closed by tests (the layout checks, an overflowing place, the drop margins above and below, the shape's shoulders and block, the selected pin on top, the feature ids in the fingerprint) and two redundant checks removed (a drop point and a translation that are not finite are caught by the finiteness of the result). **The 凸 removal and the colours and the memo were added after this run** (a small change, covered by the direct tests above: no further mutation run, in line with the lighter policy of 2026-10-21). **Not closed, and why:** the fingerprint's width, height and boundary points are determined by the cell points and the number of cells, so leaving them out changes nothing (they stay as exact inputs); and drawing only the visible pins changes nothing on the screen (the overlay is clipped), only the work. The rerun of the eight left none surviving.

## What it does not show

No routes or lines between pins, no clustering, no search, no labels, no pins in the image renderer, no geographic coordinates, no persistence in the framework (the client saves its pins), and no editor. A pin dropped is at a point of the map in map units and has no height or other meaning. How the pin layer behaves on iOS and with a mouse wheel has not been tried.
