# Drawing, Stage 1: the Height Picture and the Image Renderer

Built 2026-10-20, as stage 1 of [the drawing design](../design-drawing.md) and [plan](../plans/drawing-layer.md). It is the second library target, `FantasyMapKit`.

## What it does

- **The target.** `FantasyMapKit` depends on the core (`FantasyMapCore`); **the core does not depend on it.** Checked: the core's sources import neither SwiftUI, CoreGraphics nor the UI module; `Package.swift` gives the core no dependency on the UI target; and `swift build --target FantasyMapCore` builds the core from scratch on its own. (I did not inspect the build's module list beyond that.)
- **`MapStyle`** (`MapStyle.swift`): a plain value (`MapColor`, `ColorScale`) with defaults: two colour scales, one for water (near the coast to far from it) and one for land (lowland to mountain), and the paper colour. No UI framework is needed to make one; changing a style never changes the map. The default colours are this package's own.
- **`MapDrawing`** (`MapDrawing.swift`): what to draw, as plain geometry and colours, **the one description that both the view and the image renderer execute**: for each cell a polygon (the cell's Voronoi corners) **clipped to the map's rectangle** (Sutherland–Hodgman) and a colour. **Frame coverage (fixed after review):** a cell's Voronoi region is cut short by the boundary pseudo-points, and left thin slivers of the rectangle that no cell covered. Each border cell therefore has every edge it shares with a **boundary point** pushed outward, each end along the ray from the cell's point through it, before the polygon is clipped: the cell takes the sliver next to it, **not a rectangle of one colour laid underneath**, which would be wrong where land reaches the frame. The core map is not changed. Extended border cells overlap each other near the frame (measured below: 0.054% of pixels, within four spacings of it, on the tested grid); the later cell in cell order paints over the earlier one. This is diagnostic drawing geometry, not coastline topology. (Pushing along the edge's normal, tried first, left the corner wedges uncovered.)
- **The colours (a display policy, not depth).** Land or water is decided by the **height** (20 or more is land); the coast distance never decides that. Land is coloured by height from 20 to 100. Water is coloured by the coast distance: -1 takes the near-coast colour and -9 the far-water colour, with shades between; **0 takes the far-water colour** (so a map with no land is one flat colour); **a positive marker on a water cell** (which the original can leave beside a lake) **takes the near-coast colour**, not an unexplained dark patch.
- **`MapImage`** (`MapImage.swift`): renders a drawing to a bitmap, needing no visible view (CoreGraphics; sRGB, premultiplied alpha, 8 bits). **Pixel dimensions** are the caller's width and height, at most 16,384 a side **and at most 32 million pixels in all** (128 MB of bitmap at four bytes a pixel, before encoding; a resource policy, not a promise that a device has the memory). The drawing's size must be finite and positive and its fit scale finite and positive, checked before anything is transformed. **Aspect fit:** the map is scaled to fit inside the bitmap, keeping its proportions, and centred. **Background:** the paper colour fills the whole bitmap, margins included. **Strokes** (from stage 2) are in map units and scale with the map; the one fixed-size element is stage 1's same-colour outline on each cell, **half an output pixel** wide, which hides seams between neighbouring cells. **It is a temporary raster workaround, drawn only for opaque cells:** filling and then stroking a translucent cell would composite its edge twice. `pngData` encodes the bitmap as PNG (ImageIO).
- **`FantasyMapView`** (`FantasyMapView.swift`): a SwiftUI view that draws the same `MapDrawing` into a `Canvas`, fills the space it is given, and keeps the map's proportions with paper around it. Its seam outline is **half a device pixel** (it follows the display scale, so a 2× display gets half a device pixel as the image renderer does). **Prepare the drawing once:** SwiftUI may recreate a view's value whenever its parent updates, and building a drawing costs time, so the recommended path is `init(drawing:)`, with the caller keeping a prepared `MapDrawing` and rebuilding it only when the map or style changes; `init(map:style:)` is a convenience that builds the drawing every time it runs.

## How it is tested

- **Geometry, with no pictures:** for three templates, every cell is drawn once as a polygon with positive area **inside the map's rectangle**, in cell order; clipping keeps what is inside, cuts what is outside and drops a polygon wholly outside.
- **Coverage (corrected claim):** summed polygon area proves neither full coverage nor the absence of overlaps, so it is not used. **Instead, sample points are tested for being inside at least one polygon:** every 0.25 units along the whole perimeter (just inside the frame, on all four sides, kept at least a millionth of a unit inside because a point exactly on a polygon's edge says nothing under the even-odd rule used) and a grid of 120 × 90 interior points, on **an all-land map, an all-water map, and three mixed maps** (including land reaching the frame). Every sample is covered. The all-land image's frame pixels are the land colour (not water, as an ocean rectangle underneath would have given), and **no frame pixel of a mixed map shows paper**. Overlaps are measured separately (see "Overlap diagnostic" below). Sampling is evidence, not a proof of complete coverage.
- **Colours:** the policy table above (water at -1, -5, -9, 0 and +1; land at 20, 60 and 100 whatever the distance; 19 against 20 at sea level), a colour scale's blending and clamping (including NaN and an empty scale), an **all-water map as one flat colour** and an **all-land map as one height colour**, and a real map's fills matching the height and distance cell by cell; the drawing is a pure function of the map and style.
- **The image renderer:** the bitmap has the requested size; at the map's own proportions each sampled cell's point has the colour of its cell (about 150 samples); a wider bitmap leaves paper margins left and right, a taller one above and below, with the map centred and its colours inside; **invalid drawings are refused before anything is transformed** (infinite, NaN, zero and negative sizes, and both sides so tiny that the fit scale overflows); the **total pixel budget** is enforced (a product over 32 million is refused without allocating, and a long thin image is still possible); the PNG is repeatable, starts with the PNG signature and decodes at the requested size; and doubling the bitmap doubles the scale. **A huge drawing in a tiny bitmap is not refused:** its fit scale is tiny but finite and positive.
- **Translucent cells** are not stroked: boundary samples between neighbouring translucent cells are no more opaque than the interior.
- **The view and the renderer agree,** at the display scale 1 and at **2×** (a SwiftUI `ImageRenderer` at scale 2 against the image renderer's 800 × 600 bitmap): over 95% of about 150 sampled cell points agree within 4/255, and over 90% of the sampled **boundary** points between neighbouring cells within 24/255 (the thresholds asserted; the boundary tolerance is wider because anti-aliasing differs between the two drawing systems). A prepared drawing is kept as is by `init(drawing:)`, `init(map:)` builds the same drawing, and recreating the view gives the same picture.
- Both test suites pass in debug and release builds (core 97 tests, drawing 18).

## Looked at by eye (an example is in `docs/images/height-continents.png`)

I rendered three maps (`highIsland`, `archipelago`, `continents`) at 1280 × 800 and looked at them; the example is the third. **What I saw:** land and sea read as a recognisable fantasy map: islands and continents with green lowland shading to tan and brown ridges and a pale peak, lakes as pale patches inside land, and water that darkens away from each coast in clear bands to a flat deep blue. **The frame slivers I first saw (dots of paper colour along the right edge, 25 of 800 pixels in the last column) are gone: the outer two rows and columns of the example hold no paper-coloured pixel.** **What I also saw, and have not fixed:**
- the cell mesh shows as a faint mosaic in the sea and as a rough, blocky outline along every coast (stage 2 is meant to change the coasts);
- the water bands have visible steps, since the distance is a whole number of cell steps.

I did not judge it beyond this, and a picture cannot be checked automatically.

## Measurements (one run each, release, on the machine this was built on; not a benchmark)

| cells asked | building the drawing | rendering a 1280 × 800 bitmap |
|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | 12 ms | 27 ms |
| 100,000 | 115 ms | 198 ms |

(Extending only the border cells matters here: extending every cell first made the 100,000-cell build take 298 ms.) **The SwiftUI view's speed at 100,000 cells was not measured here; it was measured after stage 2, offscreen only: see [drawing-view-speed-2026-10-20.md](drawing-view-speed-2026-10-20.md).** The view was checked only for initial display at the sizes above and for recreating it from a prepared drawing.

## What it does not show

Stage 2 (coastlines), stage 3 (the inked look and symbols), rivers, biomes and realms; interaction (zoom and drag); and how the view performs at 100,000 cells. The edge-colour extension rule of stage 2 (colouring land newly exposed when the outline is smoothed or roughened) is not decided.

## Overlap diagnostic (added after review)

A raster test counts, for each pixel centre at 2 pixels per map unit (800×600), how many cell polygons cover it. On the six test maps (all land, all water, four generated), one grid, one run: **0 pixels uncovered; 259 of 480,000 (0.054%) covered two or three times**, all within four spacings of the frame (the deepest at 3.9: border cells reach about 1.5 spacings inward and the extension adds 2). The count is the same on every map because it depends on the grid's geometry only; where land and water meet at the frame, 227 to 258 of those pixels lie under polygons of different colours.

This overlap is a property of the ray-extended border cells, a stage-1 picture treatment. **It is not coastline topology, and stage 2 must not reuse these polygons for outlines.** If an overlap ever has to be resolved in a picture, "later cell wins" is an explicit opaque diagnostic policy, not a fact about the map.

Tests corrected the same way: the translucent test renders over transparent paper and checks the premultiplied value of one fill (0, 0, 0.5, 0.5), separating "no extra seam stroke" (actual shared-edge midpoints are no more opaque than the interior) from overlap of extended translucent cells near the frame (which does composite). Boundary comparisons between the SwiftUI view and the renderer now sample midpoints of edges two cells really share (200 of 200 within 24/255, worst 4/255 here; an observed bound, not a general anti-aliasing bound), with a separate tight check at cell points.
