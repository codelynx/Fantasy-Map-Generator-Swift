# Step 4: The Height Operations

Built in stages, 2026-10-20 onward, as step 4 of [the land plan](../plans/land-layer.md). **Stage 1: the step runner, `Hill`, `Add` and `Multiply`. Stage 2: `Pit`, `Smooth`, `Mask` and `Invert`. Stage 3: `Range`, `Trough` and `Strait`. Stage 4: the fourteen templates and the whole-map runner.** Step 5 of the plan (the templates and the runner) is done with stage 4.

## What it does

`HeightmapGenerator` (`Sources/FantasyMapCore/Land/HeightmapGenerator.swift`) ports the original's `HeightmapModule` for the tools above. **Compatibility parsing and safe execution are separate:** the text is read as the original reads it, for the template syntax; what is done with it is bounded and checked.

- **The runner.** `apply(step:random:)` takes a step line in the original's format (`Hill 1 90-100 44-56 40-60`: the tool, then four arguments) and a generator seeded from the map's seed, which the caller creates fresh before the first step, as the original's `HeightmapGenerator.generate` does (`Math.random = Alea(seed)`). A step needs the tool and at least four **non-empty** arguments separated by **single** spaces, otherwise `malformedStep`: the original's split on single spaces turns repeated spaces into empty-string arguments, and this public runner rejects them instead (a deliberate divergence on malformed input). How tabs, blank lines and comments are handled is decided when the multi-line template runner is ported.
- **`Hill`.** `count` hills, each from a start cell found by drawing a point in the x and y percentage ranges and mapping it to a cell of the square grid (`findCell`), retrying up to 50 times while the start is too high. The rise spreads outward over the Voronoi neighbours, **in first-in-first-out order**, each cell taking the neighbour's rise to the power of the map's *blob power* times a random factor in [0.9, 1.1), and stops spreading where the rise is 1 or less. The result is added to the heights and clamped to 0 to 100.
- **`Add` and `Multiply`** (the original's `modify`): add to or scale the heights in `land`, `all`, or a `min-max` range, keeping land at or above sea level (20).
- **Byte storage.** Every place the original assigns into a typed array applies `Heights.stored` (truncate toward zero, wrap modulo 256), and the algorithm's own clamp (`lim`) is applied separately, as in the original: the rise is stored as a byte, each neighbour's rise is stored as a byte, and the new heights are clamped and then stored.
- **Blob and line power** come from tables keyed by the **exact** cell count asked for, as in the original (1,000 to 100,000 in its 13 steps); any other count gets 0.98 and 0.81. The line power is held for the `Range` and `Trough` stages.
- **The execution boundary (added after review).**
  - **Dimensions and grid:** `GridPoints` now stores its width, height and the cell count asked for, and the generator takes them from it, so no contradictory copies are passed. The initialiser throws `invalidGrid` for a non-finite or non-positive size or cell count; a grid whose cells across and down, **or whose actual point count,** do not follow from its size and spacing (cells across and down alone are not enough: a slightly different width can give the same counts); a **diagram that was not built from this grid's points** (`VoronoiDiagram` keeps the points it was built from, shared with the caller's arrays); and a neighbour index that is not a cell. It throws `heightsDoNotMatchCells` if the diagram has a different number of cells.
  - **`findCell`** checks the row and column **before** they become integers. **The rule, with its asymmetry:** a finite, non-negative coordinate beyond the far edge (and +infinity) uses the last row or column, as the original's own `Math.min` does, and so it does here (compatibility behaviour, kept on purpose); a negative or non-finite coordinate throws `cellOutOfRange` and is never trapped or moved to another cell.
  - **Operation counts:** a count that is not finite, or above `HeightmapGenerator.maximumOperations` (10,000, provisional; the original's templates ask for at most 15), throws `operationCountTooLarge` before any work, for example `1-Infinity` or `1000000`. **This bounds a count, not execution time:** ten thousand hills over a large grid can still be expensive. A grid-size-aware work budget and cancellation are follow-up requirements before an app exposes unrestricted editable step lists. The original reads the count `Infinity` as one hill (`~~Infinity` is 0, and `P(Infinity)` is true); so does this.
  - **Arithmetic and the random-call order are unchanged** for ordinary template steps.
- **The original's number parsing** (`getNumberInRange`, `rand`, `P`, `getPointInRange`, and JavaScript's `+text`, `parseFloat` and `parseInt`) is ported with the original's order of random draws (`JSParsing.swift`). **The supported format is the original templates' syntax: decimal numbers and `min-max` ranges, not complete JavaScript coercion**; hexadecimal and other JavaScript literals read as NaN (a test pins `0x10` as NaN and `1e3` as 1000).

## Stage 2: Pit, Smooth, Mask and Invert

- **`Pit`:** `count` pits, each a dip from a start cell (searched like a hill's, but retrying while the start is under 20), spreading over the neighbours first-in-first-out. The depth `h` is **kept as a number** and shrunk each cell (`h ** blobPower * (random * 0.2 + 0.9)`), ending the whole pit early when it is under 1; only the heights are stored as bytes. A cell is dug once.
- **`Smooth`:** (this ports the template operation `Smooth <fr>`, not every internal method signature: the original's method also takes an `add` that its `addStep` never supplies, so it is left out until a supported caller needs it) each height becomes `(h * (fr - 1) + mean + add) / fr` clamped, where the mean is the cell's and its neighbours' heights, **summed in the original's order (the cell first)**, and every new height is computed from the **old** heights (the original maps into a new array). With `fr` 1 the mean is stored **without** the clamp, as in the original.
- **`Mask`:** blends each height with `height * (1 - nx²)(1 - ny²)` (`nx`, `ny` from -1 to 1 across the map), by `|power|`; a negative power inverts the weight so the centre drops; a power of 0 or NaN is treated as 1. **The port squares by multiplication (`x * x`); all tested `Mask` steps (nine) match the original.** A direct comparison on representative normalised coordinates would be useful and has not been made.
- **`Invert`:** with chance `count` (a count of at least 1 never draws, at most 0 never draws, anything between draws once; NaN draws and fails), mirror the map across x, y or both through the grid's row and column indices. A mirrored index that is not a cell reads as 0, as `undefined` stores in a byte array. **This is kept as an explicit compatibility behaviour:** it can only arise from an invalid grid, which the initialiser rejects, and making `Invert` alone throw would add another divergence. Whether `cellsX * cellsY` always equals the generated point count has not been checked against the original's floating-point loop.
- **How the other conventions carry over:** the operation-count bound applies to `Pit`; `Smooth`, `Mask` and `Invert` do no work proportional to a count.

## Stage 3: Range, Trough and Strait

- **`Range`** (a ridge) and **`Trough`** (a trough) are the same procedure in the original apart from a few marked places, and are one function here (`addOneRidge(raising:)`) so the draw order cannot drift between them. A start point is drawn from the x and y ranges, an end point is drawn in the middle of the map at a distance from the start (retried up to 50 times), and a ridge path runs from start to end: at each step to the **unused** neighbour nearest the end, with the squared distance **halved by a random chance** (0.15 for a range, 0.2 for a trough), stopping early if the path is boxed in. The path's cells, then rings of cells around it, are raised (a range) or lowered (a trough) by a height that shrinks each ring (`h = h ** linePower - 1`, ending below 2). Finally, from every sixth cell of the path, `rounds` steps downhill are taken, blending each cell's height with its neighbour's (`(2 * a + b) / 3`, stored as a byte, with d3's `leastIndex` choosing the first lowest neighbour). Differences: a range uses one start point (a trough redraws its start while that cell is under 20), the end may be up to a third of the width away (a trough: half), and the sign.
- **`Strait`:** a channel across the map from one side to the other (vertical or horizontal, `direction === "vertical"` as in the original). The width is processed in **two stages**, as in the original: `getNumberInRange` reads it (drawing if it is a range or a fraction) and it is capped at a third of the cells across; then, if it is **under 1**, a draw with that probability **skips the strait altogether**: a chance to skip, not a chance to apply. A strait that is not skipped runs even with a width under 1 (one ring). The path to the far side is the same nearest-neighbour walk **without the visited-cell check** (the original has none, and a path could in principle wander forever), and rings of cells around it have their heights raised to a power below 1 (`heights[e] **= exponent`, stored as a byte, and a result over 100 set to 5, as the original does). **The path has a step budget of ten times the cell count, a provisional safety policy and not a demonstrated convergence bound.** The budget is checked before each step while the end is not reached, so a path that reaches the end on exactly its last allowed step succeeds; exhausting it throws `pathDidNotConverge` before any height is changed (**the heights are unchanged, but the random stream has advanced**; `apply` is a low-level mutating runner and does not promise transactional failure, so the template runner stops on an error, and a future editor that needs rollback can run against copies of the generator and the random generator and commit them only on success). Tested with a small injected budget: the smallest succeeding budget gives the same heights and random state as the default, one step less throws, and a failure leaves every height as it was. None of the fixtures needed the bound.
- **How the other conventions carry over:** the operation-count bound applies to `Range` and `Trough`; the byte-storage conversion is applied at each store; draws follow the original's order.

## Stage 4: the templates and the whole-map runner

- **The fourteen templates** (`HeightmapTemplates.swift`) are generated **by program** from the original's `src/data/heightmap-templates.ts` by `tools/generate-templates.mjs` (so they are not retyped; regenerating gives an identical file), with each one's id, key, name, probability weight and steps. The file is marked generated and carries the original commit.
- **`HeightmapGenerator.generate(grid:voronoi:template:seed:)`** makes a whole map as the original's `HeightmapGenerator.generate` does: the random generator is seeded from the map's seed, then the template's steps run in order on a blank map. **It stops at the first step that fails.** It owns its generator and its random generator locally and returns only the completed heights, so on failure no partial map and no advanced random stream escapes, and the grid and diagram it was given are unchanged (unlike the low-level `apply`, which mutates its caller's generator and stream, and does not promise transactional failure). A second form takes a script of steps.
- **Scripts:** `steps(fromScript:)` reads text, one step per line. Lines end with `\n`, `\r\n` or a lone `\r`, so a script saved on Windows reads the same. Each line is trimmed of **ASCII spaces and tabs only** (Foundation's `.whitespaces` trims more, for example a no-break space); **blank lines and lines starting with `#` are skipped, which is a documented extension for hand-written scripts** (the original's own runner rejects a blank line and has no comments; the original's template steps are unchanged); any other line must be a step, or `malformedScript` names its line. **Any whitespace, control or invisible-format character other than an ASCII space inside a line is refused, even one attached to an argument** (`land<tab>` or `land<no-break space>` would not be `land` and would silently change the operation; checked on Unicode scalars, so a CRLF, which Swift reads as one `Character`, is caught), a line made only of such characters is refused and not skipped as blank, and repeated spaces are rejected, as for a single step. A single step handed to `apply` follows the same rule, so a trailing `\r\n` is refused there: callers with file text use `steps(fromScript:)`.
- **Random choice of a template** (using the probability weights) belongs to the original's options code, not to this runner, and is not ported; the weights are kept in the table.

## How it is tested (against the original's own generator, after every step)

`tools/capture-original-heights.mjs` runs the original's own `HeightmapGenerator` through its own `addStep`, one step at a time, on grids made by the original's own `Grid.generate`, and records **the heights and the random generator's state after each step** (equal heights can hide different random consumption until a later step exposes it). The wrapper bundles the heightmap module with the grid module. Twenty runs (**109 steps in all: 27 `Hill`, 6 `Add`, 4 `Multiply`, 6 `Pit`, 10 `Smooth`, 9 `Mask`, 12 `Invert`, 13 `Range`, 9 `Trough`, 13 `Strait`**) are compared **exactly, cell for cell and state for state, after each step**. The first six runs are:

- the original's default map (1280 × 800, 10,000 cells) with three hills (a single, a range of counts, a fixed count), an `Add`, a `Multiply` on `land`, an `Add` on a range, a `Multiply` on `all`;
- 2,000 and 1,000 cells (each with its own blob power);
- a tiny map of 24 cells with the non-ASCII seed `日本語` (the fallback powers);
- the empty seed, and a 1000 × 90 map of 300 cells (the fallback powers).

The other six (stage 2) use the original's own template syntax (`Pit 3-5 10-30 15-85 20-80`, `Smooth 1 0 0 0`, `Mask -2 0 0 0`, `Invert 0.4 both 0 0` and the others the templates contain) and variations: `Smooth` with `fr` 1, 2 and 3, `Mask` with 0, ±1.5, -2, 3 and 4, `Invert` on x, y and both with a chance of 1, 0, 0.25, 0.4 and 0.5 (**the fixture holds both outcomes of a chance `Invert`**, one that changes the map and one that does not), `Pit` counts and depths of several sizes, on the default map, 2,000 cells, a 24-cell map with a non-ASCII seed, and wide and tall maps. The last eight runs (stage 3) use the original's own template syntax and values for the three tools (`Range 1.5 30-55 45-55 40-60`, `Range 0-3 30-60 80-90 20-80`, `Trough 10 20-30 5-95 5-95`, `Strait 2 vertical 0 0`, `Strait 2 horizontal 0 0`, `Strait 3-7 vertical 0 0`, a `Strait` under 1, and others from the templates) on the default map, 2,000, 1,000 and 100 cells, a non-ASCII seed, and wide and tall maps, mixed with the other tools. **All 109 steps match, cell for cell and random state for random state, in debug and release builds, and a recapture by the wrapper's `--check` is identical.** A test confirms every tool changes the map in at least one fixture step.

**Stage 4, against the original's own `HeightmapGenerator.generate`** (`tools/capture-original-templates.mjs`, which runs the whole path for each template): the template table (ids, keys, names, probabilities and every step) is compared with the table the original's code holds; and **all fourteen templates on four grids** (the default 1280 × 800 map of 10,000 cells with every height compared cell for cell; 1,000 cells with the empty seed; 2,000 cells with a non-ASCII seed; a 1000 × 90 map of 300 cells) match on a SHA-256 of every height, the land-cell count and the random generator's final state. All 56 template runs match, in debug and release builds, and the wrapper's `--check` finds all three fixtures identical on recapture. Script parsing, a script giving the same map as its template, a different seed giving a different map, and a failing step stopping the run are tested independently.

**Small independent cases** (not from the fixture; stage 3 added: on a flat map a range raises and a trough and a strait lower (**flat-map cases only**: on uneven terrain the prominence pass can raise or lower a neighbour, so these do not show that a range always raises or a trough always lowers), a strait of width 0 changes nothing, and the strait step budget (below); stage 2 added): `Smooth` leaves a flat map flat and spreads a spike; `Mask` pulls the edge down and, with a negative power, the centre, and a power of 0 equals a power of 1; `Invert` mirrors the map, twice is the identity, a count of 1 or 0 draws nothing and a fractional count draws once; `Pit` only lowers and never goes below 0; `Smooth` and `Mask` never draw. From stage 1: `Add` on a blank map; `Add` and `Multiply` around sea level on land only (a land cell never drops below 20); `Multiply` by 1 (nothing changes), by 0 on land (everything to 20), and by 0 on everything; `Multiply` by not-a-number (the original's NaN is stored as 0); a zero-count `Hill` leaves everything at 0; and **`Add` and `Multiply` never draw random numbers** (the generator's state is unchanged). The boundary cases above each have a test.

**Invariants and fixture expectations are kept apart.** The general invariants are the array length and the height limits. That "something was raised", or that a map has both land and sea, is specific to these fixtures: a zero-count `Hill`, `Multiply` by zero or a later `Pit` can legitimately leave everything at zero, so an earlier test that assumed both land and sea failed and was removed.

## One thing to watch

`Hill` computes a power with Foundation's `pow`, where the original uses JavaScript's `**` (V8's implementation). The two could differ in the last bit; the results are truncated to whole numbers, so a difference only matters at an integer boundary. **No difference appeared in these 27 steps**, which supports proceeding, not universal numeric parity. **If a later comparison diverges, investigate the arithmetic at the first mismatch:** capture the failing base, exponent, random multiplier and value before byte storage, and only then decide whether a different `pow` is needed. A port of the engine's `pow` is not made speculatively.

## What it does not show

Every tool and all fourteen templates are ported, and the original's own generator is matched on 20 step-by-step runs (109 steps) and on the whole-map runs above, at the dependency versions in `Fixtures/original/provenance.json`; this is parity for the captured cases and four grids, not for every numeric input or size. **Not yet built:** features and lakes, and saving and loading of the land layer. `Hill`, `Pit` and `Strait` use Foundation's `pow` where the original uses JavaScript's `**`; no difference has appeared, and the plan is to investigate the arithmetic at the first mismatch, not to port an engine's `pow`. Heights from a picture are not ported. Cancellation, a grid-size-aware work budget and large-grid timings are follow-up work.
