# Water chain, stage 7a: the working heights (`alterHeights` and `resolveDepressions`)

Status: built 2026-10-21 (plan: [water-7-rivers.md](../plans/water-7-rivers.md)). The first sub-stage of the river generator: the river generator's fractional working copy of the packed heights, and the depression filling, which **attempts to resolve depressions within the iteration limit**. The rest of `Rivers.generate` is not built.

## What was built

- **`RiverHeights.alterHeights(packedHeights:distance:neighbours:)`** (`Sources/FantasyMapCore/Rivers/RiverHeights.swift`): for every land cell with a positive coast distance (`PackedFeatures.distanceField`), its height plus `distance / 100` plus the mean distance of its neighbours over 10,000, in exact doubles, in the original's order of operations; every other cell keeps its height. A land cell with no neighbours (a not-a-number in the original) is refused.
- **`RiverHeights.resolveDepressions(heights:features:packedHeights:neighbours:border:steps:work:)`**, a port of `resolveDepressions` at the pinned original (commit `8936c9c`): raises, in place, the working heights of land cells that have no lower neighbour (0.1 above their lowest neighbour, a lake counting as its own height, the feature's height unless it is 0 or not a number) and the lakes' heights (0.2 above their lowest shore cell), for at most `steps` iterations. Land cells exclude the near-border cells and are visited lowest first with equal heights in cell order (the original's stable sort). Iterations past 0.75 of the steps **close the lakes that are still unresolved** (their shore cells go back to the **packed** byte heights, the lake is set one below its lowest shore cell and closed); iterations past 0.85 stop checking the lakes. The fractional thresholds are kept (for 250: 187.5 and 212.5).
- **The bad-progress branch is kept as the original has it:** when there are more than five counts and the sum of their changes is positive, the original reassigns its parameter (`h = this.alterHeights()`), which changes only a local variable, **so the caller's heights keep their raises** and the loop ends. The port ends the loop and leaves the heights as they are; the outcome says it happened (`DepressionOutcome.badProgress`).
- **`DepressionOutcome`** (`lastIterationDepressions`: the count of the last completed iteration, 0 if it found nothing to raise; `badProgress`; `iterations`; the original returns none of these, so they are tested directly. **The original's internal `progress[0]` is not exposed:** it is a difference of counts, not a number of depressions), **`RiverResourcePolicy`** (the work per cell, and the steps: a whole number from 1 to 1,000, default 250, zero unsupported), **`RiverWork`** (the counter of attempts, charged before anything is filtered or skipped, overflow-checked on the sum, with the charges kept by part; **its public construction throws `invalidSettings`** for an invalid policy, a negative cell count or a budget that does not fit an `Int`: an unrepresentable budget is refused, not saturated; zero cells have a zero budget) and **`RiverError`** (`invalidSettings`, `invalidInputs`, `lakeWithoutShore`, `workBudgetExceeded(part)`) are in `RiverResources.swift`. The charges here: the land-cell filter (n) and sort (n times the bit length of n), then per iteration 1 for every lake examined, its shore length when its minimum is taken, **and for a forced closure two more shore lengths (restoring the shore and the rescan for the new lake height, both charged before they run)**, and 1 plus the neighbour count for every land cell.
- **Refusals, documented as divergences:** steps outside 1 … 1,000, heights that are not finite or not one per cell, a lake without a shore (the original would set a not-a-number height), a land cell without neighbours, a feature number outside the table, and neighbours outside the cells. **A refused call changes nothing** (the heights, the features and the work counter are worked on as copies and kept only on success).
- **The work policy default is 10,000 charges per packed cell, provisional.** The plan's first figure (2,000) was too tight: **the observed maximum on the fixtures is 1,647 charges per packed cell** (the runs that go through all 250 iterations without settling), so 2,000 would sit just above what ordinary maps need. The default is for development measurements, not a recommended production limit.

## Fixtures and tests

`tools/capture-original-depressions.mjs` (wired into `tools/capture-original.sh`, which now also bundles `river-generator.ts`) runs the original's own chain up to `Features.markupPack` and then `Rivers.alterHeights` and `Rivers.resolveDepressions`, on 84 maps (the fourteen templates on six configurations). For each map it records `alterHeights()` (a sha256 of the exact doubles, explicitly little-endian; whole for the small maps), and for **four working-height variants** (the natural heights, with pits every third land cell lowered by up to 12, with ridges every fifth raised by 30, and with a deterministic noise of ±10) **and the step counts 250, 40 and 5**, 1,008 cases in all, `detectCloseLakes` with the limit 20 followed by `resolveDepressions`: the heights after (a sha256, whole for 82 cases), every lake's height and closed flag, the closed flags before, and **whether the bad-progress branch was reached**. That flag is found by **wrapping the loaded object's `alterHeights` at run time** (the original calls it from inside `resolveDepressions` only in that branch; the clone is not edited): **7 cases reach it**, and **201 cases close a lake by force**. The twelve earlier fixtures came out identical.

`RiverHeightsTests` (7): **all 84 `alterHeights` results and all 1,008 `resolveDepressions` cases equal the original exactly**, including the closed flags, the lakes' heights, the bad-progress flag and the whole arrays, and the test asserts that bad-progress and forced-closure cases are present. Hand cases: the gradient of `alterHeights` and its refusals; a pit raised above its lowest neighbour in sorted order (including a cell at its neighbours' height, which counts); a lake raised 0.2 above its lowest shore cell, one already above it left alone, a closed one not examined; every refusal with the inputs unchanged; the work charges counted by hand (5 + 15 + 9 for a five-cell graph in one iteration), the budget, overflow and the policy's validation.

**Mutation checks** (46, each made and undone, run on the release build): every term of the gradient, the 0.85 and 0.75 thresholds and their comparisons, each part of the bad-progress test, the bad-progress branch not touching the caller's heights, the lake tests and raises, the forced closure's shore reset, height and flag, the land-cell filter, order, ties, comparison, 100 test and raise, the lake-height fallback, the first progress entry, the iteration limit, the steps range, every work charge, the all-or-nothing update, each validation and the budget's own limit and overflow checks. **41 were caught at first. Four were gaps, fixed with a hand test each and now caught:** a lake whose shore is exactly 100, the work charged for a lake and for its shore (counted by hand: 29, and 27 for a closed lake), and a shore cell outside the cells. **After the review** the forced closure's two extra shore passes were charged (a hand case with 20 steps reaches a forced closure on iteration 16 and counts 177 charges), the public construction of `RiverWork` was guarded, and `DepressionOutcome` was changed as above. **Nine further mutants** (each forced-closure charge removed or moved after its work, each construction guard, the negative charge, the per-part totals) are caught; **one is equivalent** (the last iteration's count read from a different variable that holds the same value). The earlier survivor about `progress[0]` is gone with that field.

## Limits

- 1,008 cases are samples; exact equality is observed on them, not guaranteed for every input. The bad-progress branch is reached by 7 of them, and the forced closure by 201.
- The work figure is observed on these maps only; the policy default is a provisional development value.
- The debug-build test takes about 90 seconds (it recomputes the packed graph and features for 84 maps and runs 1,008 cases).
