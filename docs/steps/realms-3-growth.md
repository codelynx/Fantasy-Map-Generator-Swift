# Realms, step 3: connected frontier growth

Status: built 2026-10-20; review corrections applied (genuine witnesses, short crossings, malformed-input traps) (plan: [realms.md](../plans/realms.md)). Steps 1 and 2 give the groups, the links with their closest points and the seeds; this step gives **the owner of every land cell**. No cleanup, no saving, no drawing yet.

## What was built (`Sources/FantasyMapCore/Realms/RealmGrowth.swift`)

- **`RealmOwnership.grow(in:groups:seeds:realmSeed:policy:)`** and **`GrowthPolicy`**: the explicit, validated inputs `hillFactor`, `mountainFactor`, `crossingFactor` (each 1 to 1,000), `unevenness` (0 to 100) and four resource budgets (heap pushes, pops, edge examinations, verification tests; defaults 50M, 50M, 200M, 200M). No hidden constants for the factors; the height thresholds 44 and 67 are the original's own and are fixed. The policy is checked first; then the groups, links and seeds are validated against the map at the public boundary (lengths, indices, land/water membership, counts, seeds on land cells of their own group and unique, links between landmasses with finite points); a failure is `invalidInputs`.
- **A link must be a genuine witness (validated at the public boundary, before verification).** Each point lies on the boundary of its declared cell's polygon (`pointA` on `cellA`, `pointB` on `cellB`), within the tolerance of 1e-9 of the map's larger side; the stored `distance` agrees with the segment's length within the same tolerance; and a zero-length link is accepted only because its one point is on the boundary of **both** cells (which the same checks imply). Invented endpoints, an endpoint inside its cell or in the water, a wrong distance and a false zero-length link are `invalidInputs`. (The first version skipped the two endpoint cells and let equal points bypass verification altogether.)
- **Crossing verification, before any growth.** Each link is a real segment (step 1 added the closest points). Its open segment is tested against **every other land cell polygon** (bounding boxes first): it may touch the land of its own two landmasses, **the endpoint cells included,** only at its end points (within 1e-9 of the map's larger side, so a point on an edge shared with a neighbouring cell is not mistaken for overlap) and **must not touch any other land cell at all**: passing through, touching at a vertex and running along an edge are all refused, as is geometry that is not finite. **A short crossing gets no benefit of the doubt:** the allowance is `tolerance / length` of the segment, so a non-zero crossing shorter than 100 tolerances (the allowance could then cover a meaningful part of it) is refused as ambiguous. A zero-length link (a corner contact) is usable only if no land of a third landmass touches its point, and is recorded as such. An unusable link is the named failure `unusableCrossing(landmassA:landmassB:)`, never silently dropped. Ordinary Double arithmetic: a near miss and a near touch fall on whichever side the arithmetic puts them.
- **Connected frontier growth, not shortest-path ownership.** Every seed is reserved for its realm first; proposals are queued only from cells the realm has already claimed; each unclaimed cell is finalized once (the first proposal popped wins; a finalized owner is never overwritten; later proposals are stale and discarded); a realm spreads only through its own claimed cells over land adjacency and the verified crossings. A binary heap keyed by **(cost, realm number, cell index)**, a total order, decides, so ties go to the smaller realm number and then the smaller cell and the result does not depend on iteration order. **It is not a guarantee of the globally lowest unrestricted shortest-path cost per source:** with realm-specific expansion factors a faster realm can have the cheapest path to a cell through another realm's land, which this does not allow (the chain counterexample is a test).
- **Costs in map units:** a step costs the distance between the two cell sites times the terrain factor of the cell entered (1 on lowland, `hillFactor` from height 44, `mountainFactor` from 67), divided by the realm's expansion factor. **A crossing costs** the distance from the departure cell's site to its shoreline point times that cell's terrain factor, **plus `crossingFactor` times the sea length,** plus the distance from the arrival point to the arrival site times the arrival cell's terrain factor: the land approaches are not free. **The expansion factor** of realm i is 1 + `unevenness` × r_i, r_i drawn once per realm from a seeded stream in realm-number order; **unevenness 0 means equal expansion factors, not equal-sized realms.** **No cost cap:** a lone realm takes its whole reachable group.
- **Bounds and finiteness:** every derived cost is checked finite **before** it enters the heap (`nonFiniteCost`); the directed edge count is checked against the push and edge budgets before the run, and the pushes, pops and edge examinations are counted and enforced **during** it (`workBudget` naming which); land the search cannot reach is `unreachableLand(cell:)`, never silently unowned.
- **`RealmOwnership.problems(in:groups:seeds:neighbours:)`**: an independent check, used by the tests and public: every land cell owned, no water cell owned, every realm owns its seed, and **each realm connected by a separate breadth-first search** over land adjacency and the links, a link joining a realm's pieces only when both end cells are the realm's own.
- The result holds the owner of every cell (0 for water), each realm's group, seed cell, cell count and clipped area, and the policy.

## Tests (`RealmGrowthTests`, 27)

**The chain counterexample** (A — B — X, seeds at A and B, lengths 1 and 3, factors 10 and 1: X goes to B, A stays connected); a realm spanning three islands through verified crossings, and two realms on the end islands; K = C (each group one realm) and K = the land-cell count (one cell each); a seed on a tiny island; **ties to the smaller realm number**; **the land approaches charged** (a mountain departure cell makes realm 2 win an island that a tie would give to realm 1); a mountain ridge moving the border toward the seed that must climb it, and hills costing more too; determinism and another realm seed changing a nontrivial case; **size variation, observational** (the mean standard deviation of realm sizes over 30 realm seeds was 17.8 at unevenness 0 and 41.7 at unevenness 20 on a 16 × 16 land; only the direction is asserted); **crossing verification** on hand-made links (over other land: refused; touching a vertex: refused; running along an edge: refused; a clean segment and a corner contact: usable); `unreachableLand`; a non-finite cost (sites 1e307 apart, with a mountain); each work bound named; the public boundary (invalid policy first, then seeds on water, duplicates, wrong counts, links to missing cells or inside one landmass, a non-finite point, shorter arrays, non-finite sites, groups of another map).

**Review corrections:** a link must be a genuine witness (invented endpoints far away, inside the cell, in the water, inside the second cell, a wrong or zero stored distance, a false zero-length link at a point neither cell contains or only one does, endpoints swapped); a crossing of 1e-7 on a 30-unit map is refused as ambiguous while one 1,000 times longer is accepted; a zero-length contact that a third landmass also touches is refused; **a segment through another cell of its own landmass is refused at the departure end and at the arrival end;** seed metadata of `Int.max`, `Int.min` and overflowing per-group counts is refused instead of trapping (ranges are checked before any conversion and the per-group counts are compared with the actual membership, with no unchecked sum); and the independent `problems(...)` checker reports malformed inputs (a seed or link outside the map, short neighbour lists, short arrays) instead of trapping.

**On generated maps:** all fourteen templates × three seeds × three reaches (10, 25, 60) × automatic and chosen counts, **252 cases (400 × 300, 1,500 cells): every link verified clean (no `unusableCrossing`), growth complete, every realm non-empty and connected by the independent check, areas summing to the land, deterministic.** The largest realm owned at most 86% of the land across those cases.

**Mutation checks for the corrections** (each made and undone, under a time limit): links not checked as genuine witnesses (9 issues), the distance not compared with the segment (2), short crossings accepted (1), the zero-length contact ignoring third landmasses (1), and, after a test was added because the first set missed them, the departure rule (1) and the arrival rule (1) removed. The final "a zero-length link must be shared" check turned out redundant (the boundary checks and the distance agreement already imply it), so it was removed rather than kept as untested code. **Mutation checks of the first version** (each made and undone, under a time limit): the stale check removed (fails the tie, ridge and determinism tests; the chain test alone does not notice, because X has only one proposal), crossing verification bypassed (3 tests), land approaches free, ties to the larger realm, unevenness ignored, no finite check on steps. (An earlier attempt at these checks was disturbed by a still-running earlier script editing the same file; the results above are from clean re-runs, and the source was confirmed identical to its backup afterwards.)

## Measured (release, one run, Apple M4 Pro; archipelago, 1280 × 800, reach 20)

10,000 cells: 30 realms, 27 links, **5 ms.** 100,000 cells: 30 realms, 96 links, **23 ms.** One map, one seed, one machine.

## Limits

- **Only the spanning-forest links are crossings.** They connect every group, but a realm cannot use a second crossing between two islands that a shorter chain already joins, and a realm seeded on one island of a chain pays every intermediate crossing; whether pictures need more is open.
- The values of the factors and the ranges are provisional; the tests use 3, 8 and 4 for convenience, not as recommendations.
- Tendrils and enclaves that frontier growth can leave are not repaired here; step 4's cleanup must preserve ownership and connectivity.
- A seed on a tiny island gives a realm that can only grow across its crossings against larger neighbours and may stay tiny; step 2's straight-line placement may need to change if that proves common (not measured yet).
- The over-water argument and the tolerance of 1e-9 of the map's size are ordinary floating-point reasoning; on 252 generated cases no link failed, which is evidence and not proof.
- The growth tests used 1,500-cell maps for speed in debug; realm shapes on larger maps have not been looked at by eye.

- **Where the land is large and connected, the territories look like territories:** pangea's 15 realms are of varied sizes and compact; on `continents` the big left landmass is split in two and the right one in three, with borders that follow the cost field (a visibly wiggly line over the mountains) and a realm (purple) that spans a separate northern island through a crossing.
- **Speck realms are forced, and they dominate where there are many specks:** the minimum C counts every group, and a group made of one or a few cells needs its own realm (the seed sits on it). `continents` has two one-cell realms, `archipelago` three realms of 1, 2 and 4 cells beside one of 1,334, and `shattered` has seven realms of 1 to 8 cells and one of **2,562 cells, 98% of the land**: the largest realm took almost everything while most realms are specks. With K = C there is no choice, and growth cannot even the sizes because the groups are separate.
- Tendrils, enclaves and thin bottlenecks were **not obvious** at this scale in these four pictures; the purple realm on `continents` is irregular. A closer look at larger maps and other seeds has not been done.
- What this suggests, not decides: the reach and the automatic count do not by themselves give territories of comparable size on island-heavy maps. Whether specks should be separate realms at all is a design question for the author.
