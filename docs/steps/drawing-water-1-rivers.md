# Drawing the water, stage 1: rivers

Built 2026-10-21, from [the plan](../plans/drawing-water.md). `MapDrawing(water:)` draws a water map's rivers over the land picture; `FantasyMapView(water:)` and `MapImage` show and render them; `FantasyMapDemo` has a rivers switch.

## What it does

- **`RiverOutline`** (core, `Rivers/RiverOutline.swift`): the river's shape as plain geometry, a port of the original's `getRiverPath` and of d3-shape's Catmull-Rom curve (alpha 0.1, ISC; the notice is in `ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS.md`). The meandered centre line (the core's `RiverGeometry.addMeandering`, from the water map's saved cells, flux and final heights) is widened left and right by `RiverGeometry.offset` (the width grows with the distance along the river and with the flux, scaled by the river's width factor from its source width); both banks go through the curve into a closed polygon, which the original fills. **Compared number for number with paths made by the original from the same meandered points:** 134 rivers from 18 captured maps (border exits among them), 1,880 points. The original's string is rounded twice (d3-shape keeps 3 decimals, the original's `round` 1); the port keeps full precision, so its numbers differ from the original's rounded ones by a little under 0.05 map units (**the largest difference measured over the 21,220 numbers of the captured paths is 0.0505**, a measurement and not a general bound; two successive roundings can move a value by up to about 0.0505), and the test applies both roundings to compare. **This parity is for the curve commands.** Rendering flattens each cubic Bézier into six straight segments (`samplesPerCurve`), an approximation that is not part of the comparison. `tools/capture-original-river-paths.mjs` makes the fixture (`river-paths-original.json`); it is wired into `tools/capture-original.sh`, and the other fixtures were checked identical.
- **`RiverDrawing`** (drawing layer): trims each river to the drawn land and extends it to the drawn coast, **the author's decision of 2026-10-21 (the original draws as generated; see the plan).** Against `MapDrawing.landClip` (the same rings the land fill and the ink follow, even-odd): the part of the path in drawn water before it first reaches land is dropped, the path starts where it enters; **it ends at the first segment that crosses the outline, even between two points that are both on land (a narrow lake or inlet)**; a river whose last cell is **water** and which stops on land is extended along its last direction to the outline within four grid spacings. **A river that ends on land at a confluence (its last cell is land) is not extended**, or it would grow a branch beyond the junction; the end of a river is read from its saved last cell and the packed heights (`RiverDrawing.End`: border, water or land). A river that leaves by the map border ends there (its last point is asked a hair's breadth inside the frame, never extended). **The finished fill is clipped too:** both renderers paint the rivers inside the same even-odd land clip as the land (a widened outline can reach past the trimmed centre line); when the coast is not trusted the rivers are drawn as generated and unclipped. A river with nothing on the drawn land, or cut to fewer than three points, is not drawn (`riversInWater`); a river over a limit is left out (`riversSkipped`). **The polygon budget is checked before any point is made:** `RiverOutline.polygon` computes the flattened size by checked arithmetic and refuses (nil) when it overflows or passes what is left of the budget, and also refuses non-finite numbers. **The outline's edges are filed under horizontal bands**, so a question looks at the edges of its band and not at the whole coast. If the coast cannot be trusted (its frame closure was inconsistent) the rivers are drawn as generated.
- **`MapDrawing.rivers`** (filled polygons in map units, the style's river colour), drawn over the land and under the coast ink by both the view and the image renderer; the land-only drawing has none. **`MapStyle`** has `river` (a colour) and `riverWidthScale` (1 is the original's width, 0 turns rivers off).
- **`FantasyMapDemo`**: "Rivers (water map)" and a width slider. With rivers on it makes the water map from the land map (in the background task, as the land map is made), shows the counts in the summary, and "Save map…" saves the water map (the land map inside it).

## Tests

`RiverOutlineTests` (6, core) and `RiverDrawingTests` (26, drawing layer): the land index answers the even-odd rule (a lake hole is water); the first crossing of a segment and of a ray within a reach; a river that ends short is extended to the coast; one that runs past is cut where it leaves; one that begins in water starts where it enters; one wholly in water, in a lake or with one point is not drawn; a border river is cut where it leaves the drawn land and kept to the frame where land reaches it; a stub of two points is not drawn; a duplicated last point does not stop the extension; the reach is four spacings; a generated map draws its rivers with 97% or more of their outline points on the drawn land, **and no river pixel is more than a pixel outside the land silhouette in the image; a fill that covers the whole map shows only on the land in both the image renderer and the SwiftUI view**; a river spanning a narrow lake between two land points is cut where it enters the lake; a tributary ending on land is not extended and the same line ending at water is; the end of each river of a generated map is read from its last cell (water mouths and confluences both occur); the flattened size is refused before any point is made (overflow, the limit exact, non-finite numbers); the land-only drawing and the switches (scale 0, transparent colour) give no rivers; a scale of 2 doubles the outline area (median ratio within 0.15 of 2); the polygon budget is respected; counts add up on small maps with rivers in drawn water and with border exits; identical inputs give the same drawing and the same pixels; the image differs from the land picture by the rivers, and with rivers off equals the land-only picture. Mutation checks: 26 mutants of `RiverDrawing.swift` (23 caught; three survive, below), then 17 of the corrections (segment crossings, the end kinds, the budget, the polygon preflight, the renderers' clips): 14 caught (two clip mutants only after the whole-map fill test was added), **3 survive as equivalent or redundant** (the exact-equality edge of the crossing minimum; and the two finite checks of `polygon`, which overlap: the input check catches what the output check would, and finite numbers cannot flatten to a non-finite point). One mutant (the overflow check) printed as surviving only because the script cut the failure line off; run by hand it is caught. Of the first 26, the three that survive: the sign of the ray in the even-odd test (equivalent: the answer is the same either way for closed rings), the border flag and the hair's-breadth nudge (on the maps tested the river ends at the same place with or without them: a coverage limit, since the hand-built cases pass through the nudge's logic only by coincidence at an exact frame point).

## Measurements (release, one `continents` map each, not a benchmark)

| cells asked | land drawing | with rivers | rivers alone | rivers | drawn | outline points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | 12 ms | 14 ms | 2 ms | 198 | 198 | 27,042 |
| 100,000 | 130 ms | 168 ms | 38 ms | 855 | 853 | 83,305 |

At 100,000 cells two rivers were cut to stubs and not drawn (before stubs were refused they were skipped for having too few points).

## How it looks (one picture, judged by eye)

A 2560 × 1600 picture of the 10,000-cell `continents` map (seed `kaz-1`) was rendered headlessly to look at it. Rivers show as thin blue shapes that widen toward the coast, run from high ground to the sea or to a lake, and meet the inked coast; they look plausible beside the coast ink. **That is one picture by one viewer and no more:** no claim is made about other maps or styles, and the author's own look in the demo is the judgement that counts.

## What it does not show

No lakes of the water map are drawn (the land picture's lake holes stay). No river names, types or outlines in the coast ink (the original has none; an outline is a style option not built). The look at other sizes and styles has not been judged.
