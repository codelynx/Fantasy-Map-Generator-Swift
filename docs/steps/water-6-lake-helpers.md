# Water chain, stage 6: the lake helpers

Status: built 2026-10-20 (plan: [water.md](../plans/water.md)). Sixth stage of the faithful port of the original's water chain. **These are ported and tested on their own. They are not a stage to run before the rivers:** in the original they run inside `Rivers.generate`, in its order, and the port keeps that order (stage 7). (`getHeight` and `defineShoreline`, which `markupPack` needs, were ported with stage 5.)

## What was built

All three are in `Sources/FantasyMapKit/Pack/LakeHelpers.swift`, as methods of `PackedFeatures`, ports of `generators/lakes.ts` at the pinned original (commit `8936c9c`). They work on **the heights the caller gives, exactly, as `[Double]`**: the river generator's working copy is fractional (`alterHeights` adds gradients and `resolveDepressions` raises cells by fractions such as 0.1), and truncating it to bytes would change the flood threshold and the lowest shore cell. The packed map's own byte heights go through the `byteHeights:` form, which converts exactly.

- **`detectCloseLakes(heights:neighbours:elevationLimit:)`** sets each lake's `closed`: **true** if the lake sits in a deep depression, **false** if it can overflow. A lake whose height plus the limit passes 99 is open without a search. Otherwise the search floods from the lake's lowest shore cell (the first of equals), through cells whose height is under the lake's height plus the limit, and the lake is open as soon as the flood meets an ocean or a lake lower than itself (an equal or higher lake does not count). The flood's stack order is the original's.
- **`defineClimateData(heights:gridCell:precipitation:temperature:heightExponent:)`** sets for every lake its `flux` (the sum of the grid precipitation under the shore cells), `temp` (the grid temperature under its first cell if it has fewer than 6 cells, else the mean under its shore cells to one decimal), `evaporation` (the original's Penman-based formula times its cells, rounded) and, unless the lake is `closed`, its `outCell` (the lowest shore cell **in the heights given**, the first of equals), and returns the list of lake outlets over the packed cells (a lake's number at its outlet cell, else 0, as the original's `Uint16Array`). The packed cells' grid cells give the grid's precipitation and temperature.
- **`cleanupLakeData(riverIds:)`** removes the temporary river, entering-flux, outlet-cell and closed fields, rounds each lake's height to three decimals, keeps only the inlets that are rivers of the list (dropping the list if none is left) and keeps the outlet only if it is one of them. **An outlet number of 0 is dropped even if a river 0 exists** (the original tests it as a truth value).
- **`PackedFeature` gained the fields these use** (the original's `Feature` carries them all): `flux`, `temp`, `evaporation`, `closed`, `outCell`, `river`, `enteringFlux`, `inlets`, `outlet`. After `markupPack` the lake fields `closed`, `outCell`, `river`, `enteringFlux`, `inlets` and `outlet` are unset, and `flux`, `temp` and `evaporation` hold their zero defaults until `defineClimateData`. **The state carries over between calls as in the original:** `detectCloseLakes` clears `closed` and sets it again, but nothing clears `outCell` except `cleanupLakeData`, so a lake that was open in one pass and is closed in the next keeps its old outlet cell. The tests run the passes in sequence on one value, as the capture does.
- **Index and value checks come first, and a refused call changes nothing** (the features are worked on as a copy and kept only on success). The consumed indices are checked before any change: every lake's shore cells, its first cell where it is used (a lake of fewer than 6 cells), every neighbour cell, **the feature number of every water cell the flood can look at** (it must be in the table; 0 is the original's placeholder entry and changes nothing, a number past the table would make the original fail, so it is refused), and the working heights are finite and as many as the cells. `lakeWithoutShore` is kept **at the points where the original needs a lowest shore cell** (the search after the 99 rule, and the outlet of a lake not closed): a closed lake without a shore still passes the climate step.
- **Non-finite climate results.** A temperature of exactly 80 makes the evaporation denominator 0 (infinity), and a lake of 6 or more cells without a shore has no mean temperature (not a number). **This parity layer preserves the original's numbers and does not clamp them** (hand cases pin both). **The integrated generator in stage 7 is to refuse a non-finite lake climate** with a named error; that decision belongs to its orchestration.
- **Refusals, documented as divergences:** an elevation limit that is not finite or outside 0 … 100 and a height exponent outside 0.1 … 5 (the same range as the temperature's; **package policy, not upstream limits**) give `invalidSettings`; arrays that do not fit the packed map give `invalidInputs`; **a lake with no shore cell where the original reduces over its shore gives `lakeWithoutShore`** (the original throws a TypeError). A lake with 6 or more cells and no shore has a not-a-number temperature, as the original's mean of nothing, unless it is closed.
- The height exponent is a parameter here; **the eventual settings should keep one authoritative height-conversion value** shared with the temperature (the stage 2 review's note).

## Fixtures and tests

`tools/capture-original-lake-helpers.mjs` (wired into `tools/capture-original.sh`) runs the original's own chain up to `Features.markupPack`, including the climate steps, and then the original's own `Lakes` helpers, on 84 runs (the fourteen templates on six map configurations; **53 have lakes, 237 lakes in all**). For each map it runs `detectCloseLakes` on the packed heights, on a raised copy (every land cell 15 higher, at most 100) and on two fractional working copies (a gradient up to 0.9 on every cell; the land lowered by up to 0.8, never under 20.05) with the limits 0, 5, 20, 60 and 90 and records every lake's `closed` (4,740 decisions); the fractional copies change 12 and 19 of the 265 map-and-limit decisions and the outlets of 25 and 27 maps relative to the byte heights, so the oracle does tell fractional from truncated heights; after the limit-20 pass of each it runs `defineClimateData` with the exponents 2 and 1.5 and records every lake's flux, temperature, evaporation, outlet cell and closed flag, and a sha256 (explicitly little-endian) of the outlet list; finally it gives every lake synthetic river fields and records what `cleanupLakeData` keeps. The ten earlier fixtures came out identical.

`LakeHelpersTests` (11, the malformed-index case including the feature numbers): **every pass equals the original**, through the whole chain from the map alone (our map, geography, temperature, precipitation, packed graph and features), in the same sequence as the capture. Hand cases on a path of cells: a lake behind a wall is closed, the wall lowered to the ocean opens it, the limit moves the threshold, a wall of exactly the limit holds and one just under lets the flood through; a lower neighbouring lake opens it and an equal or higher one does not; a lake high enough is open without a search, the 99 threshold (80 + 19 is searched, 80 + 20 is not), and every refusal; the lowest shore cell is the first of equals and follows the heights given; the climate of a lake (the flux, the temperature by the 6-cell rule, the formula worked out in plain arithmetic, a closed lake with no outlet); input validation; and the cleanup (only rivers that exist, the outlet 0 case, only lakes cleaned).

**Mutation checks** (44, each made and undone): both limits of the 99 rule and the elevation limit's use, the flood's order, each comparison of the flood (the skip at the limit height, the water test, the ocean test, the lower-lake test in both directions), the early stop, the visited set, the lowest shore cell's comparison, every part of the flux, the temperature (both rules, the threshold, the rounding, the divisor), every constant and term of the evaporation formula and its rounding and cell factor, the closed lake's skip, the outlet number, each field of the cleanup, its rounding, its inlet filter, the empty-list rule, the outlet 0 rule, only-lakes, and the two range checks. **39 are caught** (one of them, removing the visited-set insert, never ends and is stopped by a timeout, which counts as caught). **One was a gap, fixed with a test after the first run:** a cell exactly at the limit height (a hand case with a lake height of 24.5 and a limit of 0.5). **Five survive and are equivalent, not gaps:**

- flooding in queue order instead of stack order (the lake's closed decision is whether any qualifying cell is reachable, which does not depend on the order; only the early stop could);
- dropping the ocean test (an ocean's height is 0 in the feature records, so any lake is higher and the lower-lake test already opens the lake);
- not stopping the flood once the lake is known open (the answer cannot change back);
- not marking the start cell as visited (it is reached again once and then marked: the result is the same);
- not clearing `closed` before the search (every path sets it again).

**The correction after the review** (fractional heights, checked indices, atomic updates) was checked by **15 further mutants**, all caught (one needed a test for a cell exactly at sea level): truncating the heights in either helper, removing each of the shore cell, neighbour, first cell, feature number and finiteness checks, checking the first cell for every lake, an off-by-one, a land-cell check and a wrong water threshold in the feature-number check, removing the copy-on-success, and the flood threshold, water and lowest-shore comparisons. The fixture's captures are of two kinds: **the byte heights** (the packed heights and the raised copy, as the first version) and **the fractional working copies** (new), which are what tell fractional from truncated heights (they change 12 and 19 of the 265 map-and-limit decisions and the outlets of 25 and 27 maps).

## For stage 7

- **A non-finite lake climate is refused immediately after `defineClimateData` returns, before the drainage loop consumes it,** for every lake including closed ones, with a named error identifying the lake and the field (flux, temperature or evaporation); the integrated operation publishes no partial result on failure. The parity helpers keep their documented non-finite behaviour.
- The elevation limit (finite 0 … 100, fractions kept) and the height exponent (0.1 … 5) are carried into stage 7 as provisional package policies, with **one authoritative exponent** in the integrated settings passed to the temperature and the lake climate.

## Notes from the review

- **The feature-number guard establishes that a feature reference exists, not that the whole feature table is semantically consistent.** It does not check that a water cell's feature is water; that is broader map validation, not needed to reproduce the original helper's consumed-field behaviour. Stage 7's integrated boundary validates the structural consistency once, before calling the helpers.
- Number 0 is allowed because the original's placeholder (the number 0) has no kind and no height, so neither opening condition is true; the upper bound accounts for this table omitting the placeholder.

## Limits

- 237 lakes and 2,370 decisions are samples; exact equality is observed on them. The climate figures come through the same chain the earlier stages are checked against.
- Lakes in the fixtures all have a shore; the lake-without-shore refusal and the not-a-number temperature are hand-reasoned, not original-checked.
