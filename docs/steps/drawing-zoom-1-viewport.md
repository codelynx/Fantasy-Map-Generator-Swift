# Zooming and panning the map view, stage 1

Built 2026-10-21, from [the plan](../plans/drawing-zoom.md) (decisions: zoom and pan live in the library's `FantasyMapView` and are on by default; the maximum zoom follows the cell size, starting from a cell about 64 points across; line widths stay in map units).

## What it does

- **The viewport** (`MapViewport`): a **zoom relative to fit** (1 is the whole map fitted and centred, as the view drew it before) and a **centre in map coordinates** (map units, origin at the map's top-left, y down; nil is the map's centre). Because the state is in map coordinates, **resizing a view keeps what is in its middle.**
- **The geometry** (`MapViewportGeometry`): the transform of a map point to the view is `(p − centre) × scale + view size / 2` with `scale = fit scale × zoom`, and the inverse is defined with it (`toView`, `toMap`, `transform`, `visibleRect`). **A state that is not finite, a zoom outside 1 … the maximum, or a centre outside the pan limit never reaches a drawing transform:** `effective` clamps it first (a zoom that is not finite becomes 1, a centre that is not finite becomes the map's, **the centre is the map's own at fit**). **An unusable geometry draws only the paper:** a size that is not finite and above 0, a policy that is not valid, **or numbers derived from them that are not sound** (the ratio of two finite sizes can overflow or underflow, and the zoom limits can overflow the scale: the fit scale must be above 0 and the map's width and height on the screen at the maximum zoom finite). **`validTransform` returns the scale, the offsets and the map's rectangle on the screen only if every one is finite and the scale above 0,** and the view passes nothing else to its graphics context (given a usable geometry those checks cannot fail by construction, so they are defence in depth, not a reachable branch).
- **The limits, and anchoring:** `zoomed(_:by:around:)` keeps the map point under the pointer or the pinch fixed **until a limit intervenes; the limits win** (the zoom stops at 1 and at the maximum, the centre at the pan limit). **The pan limit** keeps at least a quarter of the view's width and of its height covered by the map (capped at the map's own extent where the view shows far more than the map), so a map cannot be almost lost with a sliver in view; at fit it cannot be moved. `panned(_:byViewPoints:_:)` moves the map with the pointer, within the same limit.
- **The maximum zoom follows the cell size** (the author's rule, 2026-10-21): `target ÷ (cell spacing × fit scale)`, with a cell target of 64 points, a floor of 2 and a ceiling of 64 (`ViewportPolicy`, all configurable and provisional). The drawing now carries `cellSpacing`. For the default 1280 × 800 map in a 1280 × 800 view: **6.3× at 10,000 cells and 20× at 100,000** (in a 1000-point-wide view, 6.5 and 20.5; for 1,000 cells about 2, the floor).
- **The view** (`FantasyMapView`): zoom and pan **on by default** (`isInteractive`); `viewport:` binds the zoom and centre to the client's own state (without it the view keeps its own); `mapIdentity:` tells the view the map is a different one, **and when it changes the viewport returns to fit** (the client says so; the view never compares a drawing; a presentation change keeps the identity and so the viewport). With interaction off, or with a viewport that is not usable, the view draws at fit as before. The map is clipped to its own rectangle on the screen.
- **Gestures** (`ViewportInteraction`): pinch zooms around the pinch point and drag pans, **composed from one session baseline** (`ViewportGestureSession`: the pinch's zoom around its point, then the drag's translation; when one ends, the baseline moves to what is on show and the other carries on from there, so neither undoes the other and nothing jumps; the session is cleared when interaction is switched off or the map identity changes); a double click or double tap zooms in **one step around that point** (a factor of 2, up to the maximum; it does not toggle); **on a Mac, Option-scroll zooms around the pointer** (a local scroll-wheel monitor that handles a scroll over the view only while Option is held and passes every other scroll on, so plain scrolling stays with the interface around the map); `+`, `-` and `0` zoom in, out and to fit from the keyboard; the view offers **Fit to window, Zoom in and Zoom out as accessibility actions**; and the demo has a Fit button (⌘0). **Fit is never only a gesture.**
- **Line widths** are in map units and grow with the zoom (as the original's SVG strokes do); the seam-hiding outline of the land and water cells stays a fraction of a device pixel (it is computed from the scale, so zooming does not thicken it). Zoom introduces **no raster enlargement**: the drawing is vector at the new size.
- **The demo** binds the viewport, **shares it with the raw-cell overlay (seeds, links) so that it uses the same transform and cannot drift**, shows the zoom, and names the generated map by its seed, land shape and cell count (a presentation change keeps the zoom; a different map returns to fit); **each successful open of a file is a new map with a fresh identity**, whatever its file name or size, so two files with the same name and size do not share a viewport.

## Tests

`MapViewportTests` (17) and `FantasyMapViewZoomTests` (10):
- **Transforms:** fit is the whole map centred as the view drew it; map to view and view to map are inverses; the transform equals `p × scale + offset`; the visible rectangle is the view's extent around the centre.
- **Bad values:** a zoom or centre that is not finite or is out of range is clamped before any use; an unusable geometry or policy does not trap; a policy that is not valid draws only the paper through the view.
- **Maximum zoom:** it follows the cell size (1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 cells give about 2, 6.5 and 20), the floor and the ceiling hold, an unusable spacing gives the floor, the target is configurable, and the view limits the zoom with the drawing's own cell size.
- **Anchoring and limits, separately:** the point under the pointer stays fixed when no limit intervenes; the limits win (the zoom stops at the maximum and at 1; near a corner the pan limit stops the centre and the result is already clamped); the map keeps the stated share of the view covered after dragging far, at every zoom, and a view much taller than the map can still pan it; dragging moves the map with the pointer; at fit nothing moves.
- **A drag and a pinch together** (pure, through `ViewportGestureSession`): interleaved updates combine (a drag does not restore the pre-pinch zoom, a pinch update keeps the drag, nothing jumps when either ends, the drag or the pinch goes on from what is on show, the session stays within the limits at every step, a session of one gesture is that gesture, and one gesture on means not idle). The SwiftUI wiring that feeds the session, and its clearing on a change of identity, is not unit-tested.
- **The derived transform:** a map and view whose ratio overflows or underflows, zoom limits that overflow the scale, a tall map and a wide map where only the height or only the width overflows, a slightly smaller case that is usable, and a transform with a non-finite number is nil; the view draws only the paper for limits that overflow.
- **Resize:** the map-space centre is kept across view sizes.
- **Pictures** (through `ImageRenderer`): at fit the interactive view draws the picture the view drew before and agrees with the image renderer at sampled sites; **a zoomed view shows the part of the map the viewport says (sampled against the image renderer's picture drawn four times as large)**; another centre shows another part; interaction off stays at fit; a viewport that is not usable draws at fit; ink that reaches beyond the map is clipped to the map's rectangle.
- **The gestures themselves (pinch, drag, double click, Option-scroll, the keys, the accessibility actions) are not exercised by a test:** they cannot be driven from one, so they are checked only through the functions they call, and by looking at the demo. **iOS touch interaction is not verified** (the scroll monitor is macOS only; the pinch, drag and double tap are cross-platform SwiftUI gestures that have not been run on iOS).
- **SwiftUI's renderer output is not stable between renders of the same view** (measured: about 14,600 of 400,000 pixels differed between a drawing's first renders and the later ones, and 2,800 pixels between two pictures that should be equal), so pictures that should be equal are compared with a bound of 20,000 pixels, which is weak evidence of equality; pictures that should differ differ by 385,000 or more, and the sampled agreement with the image renderer is the stronger check.
- **Mutation checks:** 35 mutants of the geometry and the view: 33 caught at the first run; the other two (the share required when the view is taller than the map, and the cell size the view passes) are closed by tests; then the clip to the map's rectangle and the guard for an unusable geometry, which the first picture tests did not catch, by two more. **A second round after the review** (the session and the derived-transform checks, 21 mutants): 12 caught at the first run; the others were closed by tests (the width and the height product each needed its own case, and a session with one gesture on is not idle) or by simplifying redundant code (the finiteness of the fit scale and of the maximum zoom follow from the products and the policy's validity; resetting a finished drag's translation had no effect), and `validTransform`'s own finiteness checks stay as defence in depth because they cannot be reached once the geometry is usable. None survive.

## Measurements (release, one machine, a prepared drawing, offscreen through `ImageRenderer`: **not a frame rate**)

`FMK_MEASURE=1 swift test -c release --filter ZoomSpeedMeasurement` (a water map with rivers, 1280 × 800 view, medians of renders 3 to 7):

| cells | zoom 1 | zoom 2 | half maximum | maximum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 (maximum 6.3×) | 11 ms | 10 ms | 9 ms | 8.5 ms |
| 100,000 (maximum 20×) | 99 ms | 84 ms | 77 ms | 77 ms |

The viewport arithmetic of a zoom step and a pan step is about 0.03 microseconds, so a gesture's own latency is not the cost. **At 100,000 cells a redraw is about 77 ms even fully zoomed in, because the whole drawing is traversed whatever part is visible**: zooming that map will feel choppy. The known remedy is drawing only what is in view; **nothing here says which part of the 77 ms (geometry traversal, clipping, the transparency layer of the wash) dominates, and it should be profiled before a remedy is chosen.** A live on-screen redraw while zooming, with a window server and a GPU, has not been measured.

## What it does not show

No minimap, no level-of-detail change, no rotation, no zoom controls beyond those above, no change to `MapImage`. Whether the provisional numbers (the cell target of 64 points, the floor of 2, the ceiling of 64, the quarter kept covered, the double-click factor of 2, the keyboard factor of 1.5, the scroll sensitivity) feel right is for the first live look.
