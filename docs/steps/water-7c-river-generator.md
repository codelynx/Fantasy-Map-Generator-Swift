# Water chain, stage 7c: the river generator (`Rivers.generate`)

Status: built 2026-10-21 (plan: [water-7-rivers.md](../plans/water-7-rivers.md)). The last sub-stage of the river generator: the working heights (7a), the lake helpers (stage 6) and the geometry (7b) put in the original's order, with the drainage, the river definition, the confluence flux, the lake cleanup and the river-bed erosion. **This completes the faithful port of the water chain as planned (stages 1 to 7); biomes, saving and drawing are not part of it.**

## What was built

- **`Rivers.generate(packed:features:precipitation:temperature:cellsDesired:width:height:settings:policy:)`** (`Sources/FantasyMapKit/Rivers/RiverGenerator.swift`), a port of `Rivers.generate(allowErosion)` in `river-generator.ts` at the pinned original (commit `8936c9c`). It takes the packed map and its features, the grid's precipitation and temperature, `cellsDesired` (the original's `options.map.graph.points`, which scales the rain and the river widths) and the map's size, and returns a `RiverSystem`: the flux (`Uint16` per cell), the river number and the confluence flux per cell, the heights, the `River` records, the features with the lakes' fields as the original leaves them, and a `RiverReport` of what it measured. **Nothing is changed in the inputs and a refused or failed call publishes nothing** (the work is on copies).
- **In the original's order:** `alterHeights`, `detectCloseLakes`, `resolveDepressions`, then **drainage** (`defineClimateData`; the land cells from the highest down: the cell's rain, the lake outlets whose flux passes their evaporation with their chain-lake and basin bookkeeping, the water poured out at the border, the downhill cell (a lake's outlet avoids its own lakes; a coast cell goes to its haven; otherwise the lowest neighbour), the depressed cells skipped, a flux under 30 passed on without forming a river, a new river numbered when needed, and `flowDown` with its confluences, tributary parents and lake inflows), **`defineRivers`** (the river records: cells, source, mouth, discharge, the meandered length and the widths, the parent, dropping rivers of fewer than three cells), **`calculateConfluenceFlux`**, **`cleanupLakeData`**, and with erosion allowed **the working heights applied to the packed heights (truncated to bytes) and the river beds cut** (at most 5, never under 35, cell by cell so later cells see the lowered neighbours).
- **The numeric traps of the plan are kept and tested through the end state:** the flux is a `Uint16` and **every addition truncates and wraps** (the rain is a fraction), the confluence array is a **byte while the water drains** and a 16-bit array afterwards, the river numbers are 16-bit, the stable orders of both land sorts, the truthiness of havens and of lake rivers, and that the river records use the packed byte heights while the drainage and the confluence flux use the working heights.
- **The named refusals** (the plan's): **`nonFiniteLakeClimate(lake:field:)`**, thrown immediately after `defineClimateData` and before the drainage consumes it, for **every** lake (closed ones too), naming the lake and the field (`flux`, `temperature`, `evaporation`); `tooManyRivers` before a river number that would wrap (65,535) is assigned; `riverEntriesBudgetExceeded` before any entry is appended (lake and border entries included); `workBudgetExceeded(part)` for the counted work; `riverGeometryBudgetExceeded` before a river's points are allocated; `invalidSettings` (the lake elevation limit finite 0 … 100 with fractions kept; the height exponent finite 0.1 … 5; the policy) and `invalidInputs`/`lakeWithoutShore` from **one structural validation** made first: the arrays fit one another, every cell has between 1 and 20 neighbours, every cell's feature number is in the table, a water cell belongs to a water feature and a land cell to land, every nonzero haven is a neighbouring water cell (negative, out of range, land and non-neighbour havens are refused; haven 0 reads as none, as upstream), every lake has a shore of land cells and a first cell of its own, and the grid cells the packed cells came from are in the grid arrays.
- **Divergences, documented:** a lake outlet without a water cell of its lake next to it, or a land cell whose neighbours all belong to its own lakes and whose flux forms a river, would make the original write outside its arrays; they are refused with `invalidInputs`. A river that starts or ends at the border exit (−1) would have an undefined discharge in the original; **none occurs in the fixtures** and it is refused.
- **`RiverSettings`** (allow erosion, the lake elevation limit, the height exponent: **one exponent for the temperature and the lake climate, the caller passes the same to both**) and `RiverResourcePolicy` (new: `maximumRiverEntriesPerCell`, provisional 4, a refusal policy and not a proven bound; a limit that times the cell count overflows is refused with `invalidSettings`, never replaced by a maximum).

## Fixtures and tests

`tools/capture-original-rivers.mjs` (wired into `tools/capture-original.sh`) runs the original's own chain and then `Rivers.generate` on **336 runs**: the fourteen templates on six map configurations, at two rain levels (100 and 300), with erosion allowed and not (252 runs with erosion), and with 40 depression steps and a lake elevation limit of 5 on a share of them. **Only the end state is observable** (drainage, flow and river definition are closures inside `generate`), so that is what is compared, as hashes of the exact values (explicitly little-endian) with the arrays and records whole for 100 small runs: the flux, river and confluence arrays, the heights, **25,561 river records** (numbers and cell lists), every lake's fields after the cleanup (674 lake inlet lists and 629 outlets), and the next value of the random stream (no river code draws: it equals a fresh stream's). The earlier fixtures came out identical.

`RiverGeneratorTests` (13): **all 336 runs equal the original exactly on every compared array and record**, and the 100 whole end states cell for cell; the captured runs include confluences, tributaries, rivers leaving by the border, lakes with inlets and outlets, erosion on and off and the other step and limit settings. Further tests: generation is deterministic and leaves its inputs unchanged; the river number limit is checked before a number that would wrap is assigned (the smallest limit that passes, and one fewer is refused, found by search); the work, entry and geometry budgets refuse (the entries exactly met and just passed); settings and inputs are validated; the structural validation refuses each inconsistency (a water cell in a land feature, a haven that is a land cell, negative or a water cell that is not a neighbour, a lake whose first cell is not its own, without a shore, with a shore of water, a cell without neighbours or with more than 20, a grid cell outside the grid arrays); the 16-bit and 8-bit stores (a sum over 65,535 or 255 wraps and does not stop; a fraction is truncated); the entry limit exactly met and one fewer refused; an unrepresentable entry cap refused; a lake keeps every inlet in the order the rivers reached it (appended in place, so many inflows cost constant work each); **a non-finite lake climate is refused naming the lake and the field, for every lake of a map including the closed ones.** (The report also says how many river numbers were handed out, the short rivers included, so the number limit can be checked exactly.)

## Measured (observed on the 336 fixture runs; provisional limits, not recommendations)

Most charged work in one run: **1,674 charges per packed cell** in all (`resolveDepressions` 1,647, `detectCloseLakes` 5.6, `drainWater` 22); river entries at most **0.91 per packed cell** (the provisional cap is 4); at most 7,371 geometry points (**cumulative geometry generated while the rivers are defined**; the meandered points are temporary and `River` keeps only cells and scalar dimensions, so what `RiverSystem` retains is smaller); an estimated peak of 177 KB (the policy's limits are 4,000,000 points and 256 MiB). The limits are far above what these maps use; no claim is made about what other maps need, and no timing is reported here.

**Mutation checks** (92 made, on the release build against the whole fixture comparison and the other tests): every step of the drainage and the flow (the rain and its modifier, both stores and their wrapping, the confluence marks and the tributary parents with their land tests, the river reassignment, the lake inflow bookkeeping, the climate refusals, both land sorts, the land filter, the lake outlets and their chain-lake and basin logic, the border pour, the downhill choice in each of its three cases and its ties, the depressed test, the 30 threshold, the new river numbering), the river definition (short rivers, land cells, confluence marks, source and mouth, parent and width factors, discharge, widths, the length points, the order of the rivers), the confluence flux (its filter on the working heights, the largest inflow skipped, the order), the cleanup, the erosion (the applied heights and their truncation, the 35 limit, higher neighbours, the cap of 5, the floor, the zero checks, the live neighbour heights), the two limits (river numbers and entries, both exactly), and the structural validation. **81 are caught** (75 at first; four more after tests were added for the 16-bit and 8-bit stores, the exact river-number limit and the exact entry limit; two that did not build or match my pattern were redone). **Eight survivors are equivalent or unreachable, not gaps:**

- recognising a water body as a lake by "not an ocean" (a water feature is a lake or an ocean);
- always searching the features for outlets instead of only when the cell is an outlet cell (the search finds none otherwise);
- the `max(..., 0)` of a lake's surplus (only lakes whose flux exceeds their evaporation are processed);
- the confluence mark 2 for 1 (the mark is only tested for non-zero and then replaced);
- skipping the zero-flux test of the erosion (a zero flux gives a downcut of 0);
- the empty-neighbour check of the structural validation (the working heights refuse it with the same error first);
- **the flux and temperature checks of the non-finite refusal:** unreachable after the structural validation (a flux is a sum of whole numbers; a temperature is finite unless a lake of six or more cells has no shore, which the validation refuses first), kept as defences, so only the evaporation case is tested.

**Three survivors are not distinguished by the fixtures: coverage limits, not shown equivalent:** the land test on the tributary parent when the water flows into a **water** cell that already has a river and the current river is the weaker, the "lake has no river yet" test when the flux entering it is **0**, and which lake's outlet names the basin when **several lakes share one outlet cell**. None occurs in the 336 runs. (The 16-bit wrap of the flux is also unobserved in the whole-generator fixtures, whose highest flux is 26,171; it is tested on the store itself.)

## Limits

- 336 runs are samples; exact equality is observed on them, **on the captured build** (Node, V8, architecture and platform in `provenance.json`; the river geometry depends on it, see stage 7b). The flux never reaches 65,535 in the fixtures (the highest is 26,171), so the 16-bit wrap of the flux is not exercised by the original's output.
- **Only the end state of the original's `generate` is compared**: the closures inside it are checked jointly, not one by one. If a future mismatch needs an intermediate checkpoint, it should be found with a temporary instrumented copy of the original outside the clone.
- The structural validation and the refusals are the package's; the original runs on what it is given.

## Consolidation and what is left (recorded 2026-10-21)

- **7c also fulfils the planned 7d.** The plan's 7d (the integrated operation with its budgets and refusals, compared with the original's whole fixture set) is satisfied here: the one call, the named refusals, the budgets and the 336-run comparison. There is no separate 7d code. Stages 1 to 7 of the water chain are therefore complete as a port. **Saving the exact water state and drawing the water remain unfinished.**
- **Budgets stay provisional.** Before any default is recommended, measure representative 100,000-cell cases, including lake-heavy maps and erosion on and off; neither saving nor drawing is needed for that.
- **One height exponent.** `RiverSettings.heightExponent` and `TemperatureSettings.heightExponent` stay separate low-level settings with a comment that callers pass the same value. The next generation-settings (recipe) layer must hold one authoritative exponent and pass it to both; the two-default comment is not the persisted contract.
- **Coverage limits** (not equivalent mutants): a shared-outlet synthetic case would be worthwhile if it can be built without distorting the integrated inputs; no further broad search.
