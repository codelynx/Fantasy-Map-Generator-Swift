# Water chain, stage 7b: the river geometry (`meander`, `relaxAcuteAngles` and the width and length numbers)

Status: built 2026-10-21 (plan: [water-7-rivers.md](../plans/water-7-rivers.md)). The second sub-stage of the river generator: the meandering path of a river through its cells' points, the relaxation of its acute corners, and the numbers of its length and width. Deterministic: nothing here draws random numbers. The river definition, drainage and the rest of `Rivers.generate` are not built.

## What was built

- **`RiverGeometry.meander(cells:cellPositions:options:)`** (`Sources/FantasyMapKit/Rivers/RiverGeometry.swift`), a port of `meander` in `utils/pathUtils.ts` at the pinned original (commit `8936c9c`): each cell of the river is an anchor at its point (a border exit, −1, is projected onto the nearest edge of the map, or stays at the previous cell without bounds, or is the origin if first); between two anchors that are far enough apart (squared distance over 25, or any if fewer than 6 cells) one point, or for a short step and a long gap two points, are bent to the side by a meander value that falls with the step along the river and is a quarter as strong over water; **a border exit is not interpolated to**; then the corners are relaxed. Returns the points and the index of each anchor among them.
- **`relaxAcuteAngles`**: at most four passes; **it moves existing points and inserts none**; a pass scores every non-anchor point with a snapshot taken at the pass's start (a flip in a pass does not change the scoring of the points after it) and writes the flips into the points; a point is flipped across the line of the two anchors around it if that lowers the acute-corner score of the three corners it takes part in by more than 1e-6 (a corner's score is the positive cosine at a vertex, 0 at the ends); it stops early when a pass flips nothing; fewer than 3 points are left alone; coincident anchors give no flip.
- **`projectToNearestEdge`**, **`addMeandering`** (the river's points with the flux at each anchor, the previous cell's flux for a border exit, 0 at the other points; the river starts as a stream, step 1, when its first cell is water), **`offset`** (`getOffset`), **`sourceWidth`**, **`width`** and **`approximateLength`**, ports of the functions of that name in `river-generator.ts`.
- **The geometry budget** (the plan's output policy): `RiverGeometryTotals` and `RiverResourcePolicy` gained `maximumRiverPoints` (provisional 4,000,000) and `maximumGeometryBytes` (provisional 256 MiB). **Before a river's points are allocated** `addMeandering` checks its list entries: at most three points each, with the finished rivers' points, and the **estimated peak bytes** of its temporary buffers on top of what the finished rivers retain (a river point, x, y and flux, is a stride of 24 bytes). The estimate uses the implementation's own element sizes (`MemoryLayout` strides: a point 16, an index 8, a flag 1, a river point 24) and at most 3 points per entry, for the buffers that coexist in each phase, the peak being the largest: **meander** (the anchor points, the points, the anchor indices, the water flags: 73 bytes an entry), **relaxation** (all of those still held, and for the points an anchor flag, a previous and a next anchor index, **8 bytes each, 16 together per point**, and a snapshot: 172 bytes an entry, the peak) and **output** (the points, the anchor indices, the flux list, the river points, the water flags: 153 bytes an entry). The sums are overflow-checked; a refused river changes nothing (`RiverError.riverGeometryBudgetExceeded`). It is an estimate, not a measurement (no allocator overhead, growth or compiler copies), and the limits are provisional. (An earlier version counted the previous and next anchors as 8 bytes together; the review corrected it.)
- **The resource boundary is explicit:** the public `meander(cells:cellPositions:options:policy:totals:)` preflights against the policy and the totals and adds its points to them; the port of `meander` itself is the internal `meanderKernel`, which the oracle tests and the budgeted entry points use, so no unbudgeted production entry point allocates the anchors, points and relaxation arrays.
- **Public inputs are guarded:** `offset` never indexes outside its table (a negative or oversized point index uses the last entry, as the original's undefined table entry does); the start step is checked so that adding the step along the river cannot overflow (the loop computes `startStep + i`); bounds must be finite and positive (and `addMeandering`'s width and height too); `addMeandering` refuses non-finite flux values; and **geometry that is not finite** (differences, midpoints or reflections of finite positions can overflow) is refused with `nonFiniteGeometry` before any point is published or the totals change.
- **Refusals, documented as divergences:** a cell number outside the positions, a start step below 1 or that would overflow, positions that are not finite, bounds that are not finite and positive, a water-flag list shorter than the cells, flux values that are not finite, arrays that do not fit one another, geometry that is not finite (the original would skip the anchor, or compute with not-a-number or infinity).

## Two findings that shaped the port

1. **`Math.hypot` is not `sqrt(a * a + b * b)`.** V8 scales by the larger magnitude and adds the squares with Kahan's compensation; the plain formula differs from it for **about 38 per cent** of random inputs (checked in Node: the compensated formula had 0 mismatches in 200,000, the plain one 76,513). `JSNumber.hypot` ports V8's. The corner cosines and the length use it.
2. **`Math.sin`, `Math.cos` and `Math.atan2` are V8's fdlibm-derived functions, and the captured results need fused multiply-adds.** With the system math library 26 of 600 meandered paths and 9 of 709 rivers differed in their last digits (about 1e-14). `FdLibm.swift` ports `sin`, `cos`, `atan` and `atan2` with their kernels and the argument reduction (the sizes the geometry can reach: its arguments are angles from `atan2`). The plain fdlibm formulas still differed from the captured results by about 2 per cent of the arguments, **even in Node itself**; writing the products that feed an addition or subtraction as `fma` made **all the sampled sines, cosines and arc tangents match**, and then every meander and river. **The explicit fused forms match the captured build and the samples.** This is not a claim about how any compiler chooses its contractions, and not a claim about any other build, architecture or system library; whether an x86-64 build differs was not tested. The routines were written from fdlibm 5.3 as generally known, **not transcribed from a named V8 source revision** (none was available to read); they were checked only against the captured outputs. The capture's `provenance.json` records the environment: **Node, V8 (`13.6.233.10-node.21`), architecture and platform**. The committed fixtures stay a valid oracle on another host; **`tools/capture-original.sh` now reports a provenance mismatch and stops** (nothing compared or replaced) when it is run in a different environment, unless `ALLOW_ENVIRONMENT_CHANGE=1` says so on purpose. The earlier stages' `pow` and `log` agreed with V8 on every captured value and stay on the system library (observed equality, with that caveat; no bulk campaign is planned until a mismatch gives a reason). Large arguments (beyond about 823,000 radians) fall back to the system function: **outside the geometry's exercised range.**
- New notice: fdlibm (© 1993 Sun Microsystems, Inc.) in `ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS.md` and `docs/dependencies.md`.

## Fixtures and tests

`tools/capture-original-meander.mjs` (wired into `tools/capture-original.sh`, which now also exposes `meander` and `projectToNearestEdge` from the bundle) runs the original's own functions: **600 random meander cases** on made-up positions (steps of every size around the distance thresholds 25, 36 and 64, border exits, custom anchors, water flags, every start step and cell count; the points and anchor indices exactly), **450 edge projections** (random points and ties), **360 offsets** on a grid of flux, point index, width factor and starting width, source widths, widths and 62 lengths, **3,013 `Math.hypot` values**, **5,013 sines and cosines and 5,012 arc tangents** (including exact quadrant and edge cases; a sign of zero does not survive JSON, so the `-0` cases are not in the list), and **709 rivers** (`addMeandering` over cell lists walked downhill and random neighbour walks on 42 real packed maps, with a made-up flux array), and **bulk checks of the fdlibm functions: 300,000 arguments each of `sin`, `cos`, `atan` and `atan2`** (the results kept only as a sha256 of the exact doubles, explicitly little-endian; the arguments come from small 32-bit generators whose recipe the Swift test repeats, covering the whole circle, a thin band around π/4, large arguments up to 1,000, and the argument ranges of every branch of `atan`). The fourteen earlier fixtures came out identical.

`RiverGeometryTests` (18): **everything above equals the original exactly**, including the plain-formula checks that tell V8's hypot, and the system sine and arc tangent, from the ported ones (the cases do distinguish them). Hand cases: two cells 10 apart (two bent points, worked out by hand), short steps with 2 and 6 cells, a border exit's projection with and without bounds and as the first cell, the projection's ties, the relaxation (a case that flips every pass, so that after four passes the points are back where they began: it pins the limit of four; a calm case left alone; fewer than three points; coincident anchors; the corner cosine), the offset, source width, width and length by hand, the flux at anchors and the stream start, the geometry budget (limits exactly met and just passed, the finished rivers counting, the byte formula, overflow, a refused river changing nothing) and the refusals.

**Mutation checks** (87 effective mutants: 88 made, one ill-formed mutant of mine, a change that altered nothing the code reads, is left out of the count; each made and undone): every rule of the meander (the anchors, the border exit, the thresholds 25, 36 and 64, the step limits, the cell-count rules, each bent point's signs and factors, the water scale), the relaxation (its pass limit both ways, the threshold both ways, the anchors, the cost, the ends), the corner cosine and the reflection, the projection's order, `addMeandering`'s start step, water flags, bounds and exit flux, every constant of the offset, source width, width and length, the geometry budget (each limit, the 3 points per entry, the byte formula and its parts, the retained bytes, the peak), V8's `hypot`, and fdlibm (the ranges, every fused and plain form of every kernel, the argument reduction and its sign, the quadrants, the atan reductions and combine, the special cases of `atan2`). **74 are caught.** Five were first missed by the sampled fixtures and caught once the bulk checks were added (the plain square root for a corner arm, a smaller direct range for `sin`, the plain form of the sine kernel's polynomial, the plain forms of the arc tangent's polynomial and final step): the fused-multiply-add placements need hundreds of thousands of samples to be told apart. **Ten survivors are mathematically equivalent mutants:**

- `<` for `<=` in the short-step test (at a squared distance of exactly 25 neither branch adds a point);
- the relaxation's early stop removed (a pass that flips nothing is followed by identical passes), its previous-anchor bookkeeping for anchors (never read: anchors are skipped), `cos >= 0` for `cos > 0` in the corner score (a cosine of exactly 0 scores 0 both ways), and `n < 2` for `n < 3` (the loop is then empty);
- the compensation of Kahan's sum removed from `hypot` (with two terms the compensation is always 0 when it is read);
- the plain form of `t - fn * pio2_1` in the argument reduction (the product is exact: `pio2_1` has 33 bits), and the unfused `(2.0 * x - 1)` in `atan` (the product is exact);
- `atan2`'s `x = 1` shortcut (it returns the same value as the general path) and `pi + tiny` for `pi` (`1e-300` is lost in the sum).

**Three survivors are not distinguished by sampling: they are coverage limits, not shown equivalent,** even after hundreds of thousands of inputs: the plain form of `t * invpio2 + 0.5` in the argument reduction (it can matter only at razor-thin rounding boundaries of the integer part), the careful path always taken in the reduction's quick check (it differs only where cancellation occurs, which the quick check excludes, and the tests do not exercise every such argument), and the unfused `(1 + 1.5 * x)` in `atan`'s reduction (not told apart by 60,000 samples in its range). The fused forms are kept for these because they are what the other expressions of the captured build require.

**After the review** (public guards, the explicit resource boundary, the byte accounting, the provenance) **19 further mutants were checked, 16 caught** (negative point index, start step both ways, each bounds check, the finite-output check, the flux check, the public preflight and totals, every part of the byte formula, the element size). **Three survive and are equivalent:** the width and height positivity test in `addMeandering` (the kernel's bounds check refuses the same inputs: a second line of defence), and removing the output phase or its flux term from the byte estimate (the relaxation phase, 172 bytes an entry, is always larger than the output's 153, so the output phase never sets the peak); one more was a comment-only change.

## Limits

- 709 rivers and 600 meander cases are samples; exact equality is observed on them. **The comparison holds for the captured build** (Node, V8, architecture and platform in `provenance.json`) and the samples (the finding above).
- The geometry budget's byte estimate is a formula, not a measurement; the limits are provisional.
- Arguments of `sin` and `cos` beyond about 823,000 radians are not ported (never reached).
